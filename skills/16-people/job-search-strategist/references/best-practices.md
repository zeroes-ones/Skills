# Best Practices

<!-- STANDARD: 4min — read when designing or auditing -->

1. **Build a target company list of 30-50 companies across A/B/C tiers before opening LinkedIn.** A-tier: dream companies (stretch), B-tier: strong fits (target), C-tier: acceptable for practice/interview reps. For each: company name, industry, size, why this company specifically, status tracking columns. Candidates with a structured target list start 10-14 days faster than those who browse job boards reactively. Time-to-placement improvement: 2-4 weeks.

2. **Optimize LinkedIn profile for recruiter keyword search before starting outreach.** Headline with 3 keyword clusters (role + specialization + technologies): "Staff Backend Engineer | Distributed Systems & ML Infrastructure | Go, Rust, Kubernetes." About section with business impact narrative. "Open to Work" set to private (recruiters only). Activity: post or comment 1-2x/week for 30 days before search — LinkedIn's algorithm rewards active profiles with 3-5x more recruiter impressions.

3. **Never disclose current salary — always reframe to market value for the target role.** Respond: "I'm targeting roles in the $X-$Y range based on market data and the scope of this position. I'd prefer to focus on the value I can bring to this role rather than my current compensation." Salary history anchoring costs $20K-$60K in permanently depressed compensation across all future roles.

4. **Build a personal CRM pipeline in a spreadsheet before contacting anyone.** Columns: company, role, contact, stage (researched/contacted/referred/phone screen/onsite/offer), last contact date, next action, due date. Review every Sunday and Wednesday. Candidates tracking pipeline metrics land roles 40% faster than those relying on memory alone.

5. **Conduct informational interviews before you need a job — build bridges in peacetime.** Target: 2-3 per week for the first 4 weeks. Script: "Hi [Name], I admire your work in [specific area]. I'm exploring my next career move and would love 15 minutes to hear about your experience at [Company]. No ask — just genuinely interested in learning from your path." Each conversation should generate 1-2 additional contacts. A warm referral converts to interview at 40-50%, vs <1% for cold applications.

6. **Model total compensation, not just base salary, when evaluating offers.** Build a 4-year model: base, bonus percentage, equity (RSU value for public, option value at 0%/50%/100% appreciation scenarios for private), signing bonus, benefits value (401k match, health, perks), and location-based cost of living. Use Pave.com, Levels.fyi, and OptionImpact for equity modeling. Base salary differences of $5K-$10K matter less than equity structure differences of $50K-$500K.

7. **Always negotiate — the first offer is never the best offer.** If base is fixed, negotiate: signing bonus ($5K-$25K), equity refresh, title (Senior vs Staff), start date (immediate vesting of first-year RSUs), relocation, conference budget, or a 6-month performance review for a raise. There is always something negotiable. Not negotiating costs $150K-$500K+ over a 20-year career from a single acceptance at age 28.

8. **Maintain a "brag document" recording quarterly achievements with quantified impact.** Every quarter: what you shipped, quantifiable impact (revenue, cost savings, users, latency, reliability), recognition, skills acquired. This is your negotiation evidence — "I reduced latency by 40%, saving $2.1M annually" beats "I think I deserve more" by $20K-$50K in base salary negotiation.

9. **Set LinkedIn "Open to Work" to private (recruiters only), never public.** The public green banner signals "I need a job," reducing negotiating leverage by 15-25%. The private setting reaches internal recruiters at target companies while maintaining professional positioning. Recruiter seats on LinkedIn Recruiter specifically filter for candidates with Open to Work enabled.

10. **Always be in passive search mode — take one interview per quarter even when happy.** Respond to 1-2 recruiter messages per month. Maintain your network during good times. When the right opportunity appears, you're ready. When you actually need to search, your pipeline isn't starting from zero. Candidates who search while employed and content have 2-3x more negotiating leverage than desperate candidates.
