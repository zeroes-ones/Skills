  pre-registered default (no search behind it): SPY+QQQ+IWM+EEM at N=200, monthly rebalance

    not on performance; the grid caps a book at 4 instruments, so this is the
    largest all-equity book the declared grid contains

SECTION 3 -- THE BONFERRONI BAR, AND HOW MANY CONFIGURATIONS CLEAR IT
====================================================================================================
  test statistic    : t = mean(daily portfolio return) / (sd / sqrt(n_days))
                      equivalent to annualised Sharpe x sqrt(years)
  family            : ALL 1620 configurations in Grid P, counted together
  m                 : 1620
  alpha (family)    : 0.05 (two-sided)
  per-test alpha    : 0.0000308642
  critical |t|      : 4.1670
  (for contrast, the unadjusted 2-sigma threshold is 1.9600, and the sibling
   study's m=12 bar was 2.865 and its m=56 bar was 3.322)

  t distribution over the grid: min -0.4075  p10 1.2468  median 2.3431  p90 3.3162  max 4.5962
  configurations with |t| >= critical (raw n)          : 10 of 1620 (0.62%)
  t_eff distribution (n_eff-adjusted for lag-1 rho): p90 3.4334  max 4.6616
  configurations with |t_eff| >= critical              : 17 of 1620 (1.05%)
  lag-1 autocorrelation of the daily returns: median -0.0446 (min -0.1577, max 0.0255)
  n_eff EXCEEDS the raw n_days (5260) for 1569 of 1620 configurations, because that
  autocorrelation is NEGATIVE. The sample-size correction is therefore
  LENIENT here, not conservative -- it is a property of the data, not a test.

  THE HONEST COUNT: 10 of 1620 configurations clear the family-wise bar at
  alpha=0.05 with the raw t. The answer to 'how many are genuinely positive
  rather than positive by selection' is therefore: 10.

  (1) The median configuration in this grid has t = 2.343, which already clears
      the unadjusted 2-sigma threshold of 1.96. A test on the mean of a
      long-or-flat strategy's daily returns is testing 'does the market rise',
      not 'does the rule add anything': SPY is above its own SMA(200) on 77.9%
      of this window's sessions, so a sleeve that follows it is long most of
      the time and inherits most of the market's drift with no skill at all.
      The Bonferroni bar makes the family-wise statement correct; it does not
      make the underlying null interesting.
  (2) The sample-size correction cuts the OTHER way, and this is worth seeing.
      Daily returns of these trend books have a NEGATIVE lag-1 autocorrelation
      (median rho1 = -0.0446), so n_eff is LARGER than the day count and the
      adjusted count rises from 10 to 17 rather than falling. Neither number
      should be taken as settled. What IS settled is that 99.4% of this grid
      cannot be distinguished from noise at the family-wise level.

  WHAT IS STRUCTURALLY NEGATIVE FOR A LONG-ONLY TREND APPROACH
  Read from the cells above across every configuration printed:
    * DOWN trend states are the losers, and only DOWN states. The rule is
      long-only: its only defence in a downtrend is being flat, and it manages
      that only after price has already fallen through the average -- so the
      whipsaw sessions land in the DOWN cells with a negative mean.
    * The DOWN cell that loses in EVERY survivor is DOWN x MID. DOWN x HIGH is
      negative in 8 of the 10 (the two exceptions are the gold-led books).
      The DOWN x LOW cell is thin -- its session count is in the table above --
      and is not a result.
    * Volatility on its own is NOT the problem: UP x HIGH is POSITIVE in 10 of
      10 survivors, and in several of them it carries the LARGEST mean of the
      six cells. High volatility above the average is where a trend rule earns
      its money; the same volatility below the average is where it pays it back.
    * The regime is lopsided: SPY is above its own SMA(200) on 4098 of this
      window's 5260 sessions (77.9%). A long-or-flat rule therefore inherits the
      market's drift for most of the sample with no skill at all, which is why
      the Bonferroni count in Section 3 is as large as it is.
    * There is no cell for 'no trade'. When the rule is flat the return is
      exactly 0.0, which is counted in the regime sums as a flat session.

====================================================================================================
SECTION 10 -- WHAT THIS SWEEP DOES NOT TEST
====================================================================================================
  * Short legs. Every configuration here is long-or-flat; there is no way to profit from a downtrend except by standing aside.
  * Single names, sectors, futures, options, crypto, FX. All eleven instruments are broad US-listed ETFs and the VIX index.
  * Position sizing, leverage, stops, volatility targeting, or any risk overlay. Every sleeve is 0 or 1.
  * Distributions. Every series is price-only, so TLT, HYG, IEF, VNQ and EEM are understated and the always-long comparison most of all.
  * Mixed-N books -- a book whose members use different moving-average lengths. That grid is 5^k x C(8,k) for k=2,3,4, i.e. 700 + 7,000 + 43,750 = 51,450 configurations on top of the 7,380 declared here. It was NOT run, and it would only make the multiple-testing problem worse, not better.
  * Alternative signal families (breakout, momentum ranking, carry, mean reversion at other horizons). The sweep varies N and the book, not the rule.
  * The last bar was still forming at pull time and may revise.
