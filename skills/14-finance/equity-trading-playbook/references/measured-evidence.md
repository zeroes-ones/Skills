# Measured Evidence — What the Sweeps and Frontier Studies Actually Found

This file exists so the numbers do not stay inside the study output. Every study cited below lives under
`examples/` and ships three files: a `README.md` (inputs and *stated limitations*), a `FINDINGS.md` (the
narrative) and a `RESULTS.md` (every table verbatim). This reference records **what each study measured,
with the number and the path it came from**, so a decision can be made against the measurement instead
of a memory of it.

**Every figure here is a study result on a particular window, not a market fact and not a promise.**
Tags:

| tag | means |
|---|---|
| `[COMPUTED]` | arithmetic on the study's own printed series, or an identity from the algebra, shown inline. |
| `[ESTIMATED]` | a desk choice, an uncertain input, or an assumption the study could not test. |
| `[MEASURED]` | a statistic the study computed from its own price series (the studies print their own provenance; this file marks them so they are not confused with the derived ones). |

**Nothing in this file is a profitability claim.** The headline results of the two largest studies are
negative: the positivity frontier ends at essentially zero, and 7,380 pre-registered configurations
produced no book that was positive in every year. Read those first.

---

## 1. The positivity frontier — `examples/frontier-test/`

`[MEASURED]` Primary book `R4`: equity, bond, gold and ex-US-equity trend, 25% each, window
2005-09-02 … 2026-10-14, 5 311 trading days, **20 complete years**.

The question "profitable every period" is posed as a mixing line, `r(λ) = λ·r_risky + (1−λ)·r_cash`, and
answered by where that line stops delivering 100% positivity:

| horizon | periods | largest λ with **100% positive** | CAGR there | cash CAGR | excess |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| year | 20 | **0.008** | 1.81% | 1.78% | **+0.03 %/yr** |
| half-year | 41 | 0.004 | 1.79% | 1.78% | +0.01 %/yr |
| quarter | 84 | **0.001** | 1.78% | 1.78% | **+0.00 %/yr** |
| month | 252 | **0.000** | 1.78% | 1.78% | **+0.00 %/yr** |
| week / 2-week / day | 1101 / 551 / 5311 | *n/a* | — | — | not even cash clears it |

- **To be positive in every year you may hold 0.8% of the book, which pays 3 basis points a year over
  cash. To be positive in every quarter, 0.1%. To be positive in every month, nothing.** `[COMPUTED]`
- **At full exposure the book earns +4.67% CAGR with a −14.83% max drawdown and loses in 7 of 20
  years** — 2008, 2012, 2013, 2015, 2018, 2022, 2023. `[MEASURED]`
- **Daily, weekly and biweekly have no endpoint at all, because cash itself was not positive every
  day.** In this window **21 of 5 311 days** have a non-positive cash accrual: 8 days when the 3-month
  T-bill yield printed exactly 0.00%, and **11 days when it printed negative**, from −0.0025% to
  −0.0406% (2015-09-18 … 2015-10-14 and 2020-03-25 … 2020-03-30). Under a strict reading a cash-only
  plan fails its own target on those 21 days, so the floor at daily resolution is 99.6%, not 100%.
  `[MEASURED — real T-bill yields]`
- **The price is not uniform.** Going from 70% to 75% of years costs 78 bp of CAGR; 75%→95% costs 54 bp
  for four times as many years; the last five points (95%→100%) cost 49 bp and buy exactly one calendar
  year (2015). `[MEASURED]`
- **The endpoint is one year and 0.0056 percentage points wide.** At λ = 0.008 the book's 2015 returns
  `+0.0038%`; at λ = 0.009 it returns `−0.0017%`. That knife edge is the whole difference between
  "positive in every year" and not. `[MEASURED]`
- **Costs attack the return, not the positivity.** At a 20 bp round trip the endpoint moves by one
  thousandth of exposure (0.008 → 0.007) while CAGR at λ = 1 falls ~0.9 pp. A cost-reduction campaign
  cannot buy back the losing years. `[MEASURED]`

## 2. The law: positivity is bought with Sharpe — `examples/frontier-test/`

The frontier is generated by one relation, established by the predecessor study and re-used here:

```
P(positive period) ≈ Φ( annual_Sharpe · √period_fraction )
```

`[COMPUTED]`. It says the *only* two things that move the fraction of positive periods are the Sharpe
ratio and how long the period is — and the period length enters as a square root, so a shorter horizon
is a weaker lever than the arithmetic looks. Derived at `annual_Sharpe = 0.60`:

| period | `period_fraction` | `0.60·√fraction` | `Φ(·)` |
|---|---|---|---|
| day | 1/252 | 0.0378 | **51.5%** |
| week | 1/52 | 0.0832 | 53.3% |
| month | 1/12 | 0.1732 | **56.9%** |
| quarter | 1/4 | 0.3000 | **61.8%** |
| half-year | 1/2 | 0.4243 | 66.4% |
| year | 1 | 0.6000 | **72.6%** |

`[COMPUTED]`.

**And the measured yearly rate sits below the prediction.** The four-sleeve book's own Sharpe is
**0.604** (`examples/weather-test/`, book F at λ = 1: CAGR 4.67%, maxDD −14.83%) against a measured
positivity of **65% of years (13 of 20)**, where the normal relation predicts **72.6% — about 14.5 of
20 years**. `[MEASURED]` The 1.5-year gap is the fat tail: the losses arrive as a few large down years
rather than spread thinly, so the left tail carries more weight than a Gaussian of the same Sharpe
would give it. **Selecting a longer timeframe on its own does not buy positivity** — the relation moves
only with Sharpe, and the timeframe enters as `√fraction`.

The same relation regenerates the narrow-universe trade-off table in §5, so it is not an artefact of one
book. `examples/narrow-universe-test/FINDINGS.md` states it explicitly and uses it.

## 3. Nothing reached 100% of years — `examples/sweep-test/`

`[MEASURED]` **7 380 pre-registered configurations** across three grids (P: 1 620, L: 150, X: 5 610 —
declared counts asserted against the counts actually run, and re-enumerated independently by recursion).
Window for the main grid 2005-11-14 … 2026-10-14, 5 261 sessions, 20.91 years.

- **Not one configuration, in any grid, is positive in every complete year.** The best fraction of
  positive years at a positive CAGR is **18 of 20 = 90.00%**, reached by `N100-monthly-QQQ+TLT+GLD`
  with **CAGR 6.79%, Sharpe 0.8740, max drawdown −11.37%**, and its two losing years are
  **2015 (−5.83%) and 2022 (−7.77%)**. `[MEASURED]`
- **A grid this wide produces a green row by construction.** Of the 1 620 configurations: **98.7% have a
  positive CAGR** and **99.6% a positive Sharpe**; the **median Sharpe is 0.5129** and the
  **p10 configuration is still up 2.22% a year**. The only informative columns are the tails.
  `[MEASURED]`
- **More iteration makes it worse, monotonically.** Configurations clearing the Bonferroni bar for their
  own grid size: **10 of 1 620** (0.62%), **1 of 150** (0.67%), **0 of 5 610** (0%). The share of the
  grid that survives falls as the grid grows. `[MEASURED]`
- **Most of a large grid looks significant by construction.** The grid's **median `t` is already 2.3431**
  — past the unadjusted 1.96 — while the Bonferroni critical value at m = 1 620 is **4.1670**. `[MEASURED]`
- **The ten survivors are not ten discoveries.** All ten contain GLD, nine contain QQQ; IWM, EEM, EFA and
  VNQ appear in **none**. If they had been drawn at random from the grid, the chance that every one
  contained gold would be about `(640/1620)^10 ≈ 9 × 10^-5`. The search found "hold gold and the
  Nasdaq", which is a bet on two assets over one window. `[COMPUTED]`
- **One implementation defect was found and fixed** after the first run (in the regime series, without
  changing the regime definition); every headline and out-of-sample number is identical before and after
  it. `[MEASURED]`

## 4. Long-only trend is structurally negative in the DOWN state — `examples/sweep-test/`

`[MEASURED]` Regime cells over the ten Bonferroni survivors:

- **UP × LOW, UP × MID and UP × HIGH are positive in 10 of 10 survivors.** Being long in an uptrend is
  the entire edge, in every volatility tercile.
- **DOWN × MID is negative in 10 of 10** — the only universally negative cell with a usable sample.
- DOWN × HIGH is negative in 8 of 10 (gold carries the other two). DOWN × LOW is a null cell: the
  condition occurs on two sessions in 20.9 years and is not a result.
- The mechanism is **structural, not statistical**: the rule is long-only, so its only defence in a
  downtrend is being flat, and it can only become flat **after** price has already crossed the average.
  The sessions where price whipsaws around the average are exactly the ones it cannot win.
- A long-or-flat sleeve also inherits the market's drift on **77.9%** of this window's sessions with no
  skill at all, because SPY is above its own SMA(200) that often. That is why the survivor count above is
  as large as ten, and why the Bonferroni correction makes the *family-wise* statement honest without
  making the underlying null a test of skill.

## 5. Narrowing the universe destroys diversification — `examples/narrow-universe-test/`

`[MEASURED]` Primary window W14 = 2013-03-07 … 2026-10-14 (3 422 days, 13.6 years); long window W10 =
2005-09-02 … 2026-10-14 (21.1 years), the one containing 2008.

- **SPY, QQQ and IWM are one bet.** Mean pairwise correlation **0.875 yearly / 0.838 daily**; three
  equally weighted names at that correlation are worth **1.12 independent bets** — a rounding error away
  from one. Against the sibling regime study's 0.666–0.887 that the predecessor called "one bet taken
  with three different exits", three index ETFs *are* that same bet. `[MEASURED]`
- **The narrow book is still beaten by simply buying SPY.** Buy-and-hold SPY returned **12.513%** on W14
  against the 3-index trend book's **9.858%** (and 9.104% against 7.457% on W10) — with the drawdown the
  trend rule exists to avoid (−34.10% vs −19.72%). On return the order is: **buy & hold the broad index >
  narrow 3-index trend book > cross-asset trend book.** `[MEASURED]`
- **Narrowing moves the "never lose a year" frontier by ~0.03 percentage points** of annual excess once
  the window contains a real bear market (W10: exposure 0.80% → 0.90%, excess 0.025% → 0.057%). On the
  window with no 2008 (W14) the same comparison gives +0.161 pp — five times larger, and a window
  artefact. The frontier is set by the *rule* and the *cash sliver*, not by how many tickers the rule is
  applied to. `[MEASURED]`
- **A basket of constituents diversifies better than the index** — mean pairwise correlation
  **0.348 daily / 0.235 yearly**, **2.53 effective bets** against 1.12. The *return* advantage of that
  basket is survivorship-contaminated and must not be quoted as a result; the correlation figure is not.
  `[MEASURED]`

## 6. Two sleeve lessons measured the hard way

**A trend rule on VIX itself loses money while VIX rises — `examples/weather-test/`.** `[MEASURED]`
Sleeve `E`, long the VIX index whenever VIX is above its own 50-day average, earned **−86.90% a year
compounded on the days it was long** — over 2 100 days — while the VIX index itself **rose 24.24%**
(13.57 → 16.86) over the same window. The loss is the roll and the mean reversion, not the direction of
the index. Consequences measured in the same study: the pre-registered five-sleeve book lost money in
**17 of 20 complete years** with a worst year of **−25.27%** and a **−86.08%** drawdown, and sleeve E was
negative in **all 17** of them. **Diversification worked and the book still failed** — mean pairwise
yearly correlation was **+0.016** against the sibling's 0.666–0.887, which is about as decorrelated as a
book can be built, and one sleeve with negative drift still sank it. *Correlation is necessary for a
diversified book; it is not sufficient, and it is not evidence of edge.*

**A paid tail hedge repairs the crash and destroys the carry — `examples/index-options-test/`
(cross-playbook; the equity studies contain no hedge sleeve).** `[MODELED]` A credit-funded long-put
variant, sized so the hedge debit equals the collected premium, **halved the 2008 damage
(−11.97% → −5.63% of the risk budget)** and turned 2020 positive (−1.09% → +0.07%), and it cost
**2.72 percentage points of CAGR** and four points of positive years. The literal (unfunded) version of
the same hedge did the opposite of what it looks like it does: `+9.59%` in 2008 but `−36.24%` in 2020,
because its cost dominates a year without a sustained crash. **The hedge repairs the tail and destroys
the carry; both readings are in that study's `RESULTS.md` and neither is a recommendation.**

## 7. What these numbers are not

1. **Every series here is price-only — dividends are excluded.** Bonds, credit and gold are all
   understated by this, which understates the return of the always-long sleeves and therefore
   **understates the bar the frontier has to clear**. A total-return version would raise the return
   available at the risky end; it would not move the endpoint much, because the endpoint is a statement
   about loss years rather than about average return. `[ESTIMATED — the size of the understatement was
   not measured]`
2. **Not out-of-sample claims.** "100% of years positive" is a statement about the 20 or 32 periods in
   the window. Twenty years is enough to see a tail and not enough to settle anything.
3. **Not free of selection.** Both the frontier and the sweep numbers are selected on the same search
   that produced them; the sweep's own §4 and §9 are the honest statement of that, and its p10 and worst
   configurations are reported precisely so a "best of N" reading is impossible.
4. **One rule family only.** Every book in this file is an SMA(N) long-or-flat rule on a set of assets.
   Mean reversion, carry, cross-asset relative value and held long volatility are absent. Nothing here
   shows a different sleeve shape has fewer loss years.
5. **Limitations are stated in each study's own `README.md`, not only here** — read
   `examples/frontier-test/README.md`, `examples/sweep-test/README.md`,
   `examples/narrow-universe-test/README.md` and `examples/weather-test/README.md` before acting on any
   number, and `FINDINGS.md` for the study's own statement of what it refuses to conclude.
6. **This skill's own arithmetic is elsewhere.** `references/what-this-cannot-promise.md`,
   `references/overfitting-by-iteration.md` and `references/the-iteration-protocol.md` hold the
   identities and the guards; this file holds *results*, and a result is only as good as the window that
   produced it.
