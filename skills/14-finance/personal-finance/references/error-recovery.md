# Personal Finance — Error Recovery

## Symptom → Root Cause → Fix

| Symptom | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|---------|-----------|-----|--------|
| Budget looks balanced on paper but always overspends | Budget categories too aggregated. "Miscellaneous" hides real spending. Irregular expenses not annualized and divided by 12. | Track every expense for 90 days. Create categories that match your actual spending patterns. Annualize irregular costs: car repair = $1,200/yr → $100/mo. | **Aggregated categories hide leaks.** You can't fix what you don't measure. The "miscellaneous" category is where budgets go to die. |
| Debt snowball/avalanche calculator gives wrong payoff date | Interest capitalization not modeled. Minimum payment changes as balance drops. Extra payments not applied to principal. | Use amortization schedule with actual terms. Verify: each extra payment reduces principal by exactly that amount. Account for variable-rate adjustments. | **Debt calculators are only as good as their assumptions.** Miss one interest capitalization and your payoff date is off by months. |
| Retirement projection shows comfortable retirement but reality is tight | Assumed 7% real return (optimistic). Didn't account for sequence-of-returns risk. Long-term care costs not included. | Model at 4-5% real return for equity-heavy, 2-3% for balanced. Monte Carlo with sequence risk. Include $200K+ for end-of-life care. | **Retirement projections are sensitive to the first 5 years of returns.** A bear market in years 1-3 of retirement can permanently reduce sustainable withdrawal rate by 30%+. |
| Tax optimization strategy triggers audit or penalty | Wash sale violation on tax-loss harvesting. IRA contribution while ineligible. HSA distribution without qualified expense documentation. | Know the rules before acting: wash sale = 30 days before/after. IRA income limits by filing status. Save all HSA receipts digitally. | **Tax optimization requires rule compliance, not just math.** A $500 tax saving that triggers a $2,000 penalty + audit is a net loss. When in doubt, consult a CPA. |
| Insurance coverage looks adequate until claim denied | Coverage limits too low. Exclusions not read. Umbrella policy not layered correctly. | Audit annually: auto liability ≥ net worth, home replacement cost (not market value), umbrella ≥ net worth. Read exclusions section. | **Insurance is tested when you need it, not when you buy it.** The wrong time to discover a coverage gap is during a claim. Audit before you need it. |
