# Fundamental Analyst — Error Recovery

## Symptom → Root Cause → Fix

| Symptom | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|---------|-----------|-----|--------|
| Valuation model shows stock is 40% undervalued but price keeps falling | Terminal value assumption too aggressive (>80% of DCF value). Growth rate exceeds GDP + inflation indefinitely. | Cap terminal growth rate at risk-free rate (2-3%). Terminal value should be <70% of total DCF. Cross-validate with multiple-based valuation. | **Terminal value is where DCF models go to die.** Every 1% change in terminal growth can swing valuation by 20%. Be conservative or be wrong. |
| Earnings quality analysis misses accounting red flags | Focusing on GAAP earnings without analyzing cash flow statement. Ignoring notes to financial statements. | Always compare: Net Income vs. Operating Cash Flow vs. Free Cash Flow. Divergence >20% over 3 years = red flag. Read all footnotes about revenue recognition and one-time items. | **Earnings are an opinion. Cash flow is a fact.** Companies that report growing earnings with declining cash flow are almost always using aggressive accounting. |
| Screening produces too many/few results | Filters too loose (500+ results) or too tight (<5 results). | Iterate: start broad (market cap, sector), add liquidity (volume), then fundamentals (P/E, ROE, debt/equity). Target 20-50 actionable candidates. | **Screen output = signal/noise ratio of your filters.** Too many = no filtering. Too few = you're missing opportunities or overfitting to historical winners. |
| Dividend analysis misses sustainability issues | Using trailing dividend yield without checking payout ratio, free cash flow, or debt maturity schedule. | Dividend safety checklist: payout ratio <60% (earnings), <50% (FCF), debt/EBITDA <3x, no near-term debt wall. | **High yield + high payout ratio = dividend cut incoming.** The market prices in the cut before it's announced — yield spikes are warning signs, not bargains. |
| Value trap: stock looks cheap on P/E but never recovers | Structural decline not cyclical downturn. Industry disruption not mean-reversion. | Distinguish cyclical (semiconductor cycles, commodity cycles) from structural (retail → ecommerce, print → digital). Structural cheap can stay cheap forever. | **Cheap ≠ value.** Value requires a catalyst for mean reversion. Without a catalyst, cheap is just a value trap with a low P/E. |
