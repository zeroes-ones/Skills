# Accounting Red Flags — Historical Case Studies

## Category 1: Revenue Manipulation
| Flag | Case Study | Cost |
|------|-----------|------|
| Channel stuffing (receivables +40% vs revenue +10%) | Diamond Foods (2011): shipped excess inventory to distributors, booked as revenue. SEC investigation, stock -60% | $500M market cap loss |
| Round-tripping (selling to customer who sells back) | Sunbeam (1997): Chainsaw Al Dunlap booked "sales" to distributors who could return unsold product. SEC suit, bankruptcy. | $3B market cap → $0 |
| Bill-and-hold (revenue before delivery) | Lucent (2000): $679M in revenue from equipment stored in Lucent warehouses | $200B market cap loss |

## Category 2: Expense Manipulation
| Flag | Case Study | Cost |
|------|-----------|------|
| Capitalizing operating expenses | WorldCom (2002): $3.8B in line costs capitalized as "capital expenditures." Largest fraud in US history at the time. | $180B market cap → $0, CEO 25 years prison |
| Cookie jar reserves | Freddie Mac (2003): $5B earnings misstatement via reserve manipulation. | $30B market cap loss |
| Extending depreciation lives | Waste Management (1998): $1.7B fraud via extending truck depreciation lives. | $6B restatement |

## Category 3: Off-Balance-Sheet
| Flag | Case Study | Cost |
|------|-----------|------|
| SPEs hiding debt | Enron (2001): $38B in debt hidden in special purpose entities. | $74B → $0, Arthur Andersen dissolved |
| Repo 105 (temporary debt reduction) | Lehman Brothers (2008): $50B temporarily moved off balance sheet at quarter-end. | Bankruptcy triggered global financial crisis |

## Category 4: Cash Flow vs. Earnings
| Flag | Case Study | Cost |
|------|-----------|------|
| NI growing, OCF shrinking | Luckin Coffee (2020): fabricated $310M in revenue. NI looked great; cash flow never materialized. | Stock -90%, delisted |
| FCF negative 5+ years | Wirecard (2020): €1.9B "cash" didn't exist. 10+ years of negative FCF despite reported profits. | Insolvency, CEO arrested |

## Decision Rule
Any ONE of these flags = investigate deeper. Two+ flags = walk away. There are 10,000+ investable securities — no need to buy companies with red flags.
