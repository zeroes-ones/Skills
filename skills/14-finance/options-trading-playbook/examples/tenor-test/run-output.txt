
==============================================================================
TENOR TEST -- does tenor buy edge, or only change the clock?
==============================================================================
Pre-registered.  Deterministic.  No RNG, no clock, no network.

READ THIS BEFORE ANY NUMBER: every option price below is [MODELED].
Historical option quotes are not available from the sources used.
Legs are priced with Black-Scholes on a VIX-derived implied vol and
settled against the REAL index close on the expiry session.  The
model has: no quote spread, NO SKEW, NO TERM STRUCTURE (one vol for
every strike AND every tenor), NO EARLY EXERCISE AND NO ASSIGNMENT
(SPY/QQQ/IWM options are American and physically settled; they are
priced here as European and cash-settled), no pin risk, no dividends,
no margin, no liquidity limit, NO LEGGING RISK, unrounded strikes.
A flat per-contract cost IS charged; the QUOTE is still a mid.

STRUCTURES -- the brief enumerates SEVEN, not six (2 one-leg, 3
two-leg, 2 four-leg).  All seven are run; see README section 3.
  id  structure                                        option legs  declared width W
  --  -----------------------------------------------  -----------  ----------------
  S1  long call ~50d                                   1            n/a
  S2  cash-secured put ~16d                            1            R/share
  S3  short put spread ~16d / 5-wide                   2            5.0
  S4  bull call debit spread ~50d/25d                  2            5.0
  S5  covered call: long underlying + short ~30d call  2            S_entry
  S6  iron condor ~16d shorts / 5-wide wings           4            5.0
  S7  iron butterfly ATM short / 5-wide wings          4            5.0
W is DECLARED, not derived per trade: $5.00 for the four wing
structures; for the cash-secured put W is the short strike K (the
cash actually secured); for the covered call W is the underlying
entry price (the notional the call is written against).  A naked
long call has NO width at all -- only its debit/notional is
meaningful, and its 'credit' is a DEBIT.

ENTRY PROGRAMME: sequential and non-overlapping.  The first trade
opens on the second session of the window, exits on the first
session on or after entry + tenor days, and the next trade opens on
the session after that expiry.  The sleeve is always in the market.
Entry is at the OPEN, strikes are solved from the SIGNAL close (the
prior session), and settlement is at INTRINSIC against the expiry
session's REAL close.  Nothing is managed: no stop, no target, no
roll.

R = the structure's own modelled MAXIMUM LOSS per contract (exact
piecewise-linear minimum, not a grid).  Every structure here is
BOUNDED: the covered call's short call is covered by stock, so no
stated-stop normalisation is needed anywhere in this study.

COST (headline LOW) = ($0.65 + $0.025 x 100) per ticket per
contract-leg, one ticket per leg: $3.15 per leg, so $3.15 / $6.30 /
$12.60 per unit for 1 / 2 / 4 legs.  The covered call also pays 5 bps
round trip on its stock leg.  HIGH doubles the spread widening;
RT_LOW doubles every ticket (the round-trip convention of
`cost-drag-per-leg-and-per-rung.md`).  A LEG THAT EXPIRES HAS NO
CLOSING TICKET, which is why one ticket per leg is the headline.

E is the GROSS expectancy in R (settlement P&L / R, before cost), so
that the brief's identity E_net = E - c_R holds exactly.  p and b are
computed on the same gross series; p_net is reported beside them.

n_available = trades POOLED over SPY+QQQ+IWM (the sibling studies'
convention); n_per_index = n_available / 3.  n_req = 4 (sd/|E|)^2.

  HOW TO READ THAT TABLE.  The grid splits cleanly, and the split is
  NOT the one the brief anticipated:
    * for the four 5-WIDE structures (S3, S4, S6, S7) the credit RISES
      with tenor as a fraction of the width (S3: 0.055 -> 0.211; S6:
      0.119 -> 0.303) while R = width - credit FALLS (S3: $472 -> $394;
      S6: $441 -> $349).  The brief's mechanism -- credit/width and R
      moving in OPPOSITE directions -- is CONFIRMED.  Its implied
      conclusion is CONTRADICTED: c_R RISES with tenor (S3: 0.0136 ->
      0.0163; S6: 0.0294 -> 0.0379), because the credit grows faster
      than R shrinks.  The mechanism is the fixed-dollar wing: at 7 DTE
      a $5 wing is 1.44 sigma of the terminal distribution and sits far
      out in the tail where density is low; at 730 DTE it is 0.15 sigma
      and sits near the money where density is high, so the same $5
      captures far more premium per dollar of width.
    * for the structures whose R is NOT a wing and DOES scale with the
      index and with sqrt(T) -- S1 and S4 (R = the debit) and weakly S2
      and S5 (R = the cash secured / the notional) -- c_R FALLS with
      tenor, by up to 8.5x (S4: 0.0877 -> 0.0140; S1: 0.0276 ->
      0.0034), because the ticket cost is a constant $3.15-$6.30 while
      R grows.
  Cost is NOT flat in tenor.  It rises for the width-defined structures
  and falls for the index-scaled ones, and the reason is the same in
  both cases: cost is a fixed dollar amount and R is not.

  THE ONE THING THIS STUDY ADDS TO THAT CALENDAR, and it is a

  buckets carry different hard deadlines (21 DTE vs 45 DTE) and
  different roll windows (21-30 vs 60-75).  This is a real ambiguity
  in the owning skill, not in this study; section 1 declares the
  assignment used here (60 -> quarterlies).

12. THE 0DTE QUESTION -- ANALYTIC, NOT BACKTESTED
==============================================================================

NO multi-year 0DTE backtest is reported from these bars, and none
should be believed from daily bars: 0DTE P&L is decided by the
INTRADAY PATH, and a daily open/high/low/close cannot see it.  What
CAN be derived exactly is the scaling.

Stated inputs for the analytic block only: S = K = 100 (at the money), sigma = 20%, r = q = 0 (the reduced form the owning skill derives its ladder from).  T_long = 30 days, T_short = 0.25 days.

  quantity                 30 DTE    0.25 DTE  ratio     derived expectation
  -----------------------  --------  --------  --------  -----------------------
  gamma (dDelta per $1)    0.069548  0.762176  10.959x   sqrt(30/0.25) = 10.954
  |theta| per DAY ($)      0.038109  0.417630  10.959x   same 1/sqrt(T)
  ATM option value ($)     2.2872    0.2088    0.091x    1/sqrt(T) the other way
  |theta| per day / value  0.016662  1.999995  120.033x  T_long/T_short = 120.0

  [COMPUTED]  Gamma_ATM ~ 1/sqrt(T), so moving from 30 DTE to 0.25 DTE
  multiplies the ATM gamma by sqrt(30/0.25) = 10.954.  The model returns
  10.959 -- an exact match, because the pricer IS Black-Scholes.
  [COMPUTED]  |theta| per day carries the same 1/sqrt(T), so the same
  10.959 multiplies the DAILY DECAY IN DOLLARS.  But the position's VALUE
  falls by the same factor, so the daily decay as a FRACTION OF THE
  PREMIUM scales as 1/T: 120.0x, i.e. 1.666% per day at 30 DTE against
  200.0% per day at 0.25 DTE.  A 0.25-DTE option that is worth anything
  at all is worth it for hours, not days.  That is the honest 0DTE
  statement: it is not 'more theta', it is 'the same theta in a tenth
  of the time, at ten times the gamma'.

  [COMPUTED]  The same identity fixes the shape of the risk: one 1% move in the underlying
  changes the position's delta by 0.76 at 0.25 DTE against 0.07 at
  30 DTE -- the same move re-writes ten times as much of the hedge at
  0.25 DTE.  A delta band that is wide enough at 30 DTE is crossed by
  an ordinary morning move at 0.25 DTE.

THE COST-PER-TICKET ARITHMETIC AGAINST A SMALL CREDIT.  A 0DTE ticket
is the same ticket as any other -- the fee does not shrink with the
credit.  At the headline LOW setting a 2-leg ticket costs
2 x ($0.65 + $0.025 x 100) = $6.30, and a sub-$0.20 credit is the
regime a 0DTE trader is actually in whenever the strikes are far enough
out to have any room:

  0.05         5.00    5.00      495  6.30        126.0%         0.0127   0.0228

  [COMPUTED]  Read the 'cost / credit' column: on a $0.05 credit the
  round-trip ticket is 126% of the ENTIRE premium at LOW prices and
  226% at HIGH prices.  On a $0.20 credit it is 32% / 56%.
  [COMPUTED]  The c_R column is much kinder (1.3% to 7.9% of R) because
  a 1-to-5-wide wing makes R large relative to the cost.  BOTH views
  are true and they answer different questions: cost as a share of
  PREMIUM is what a high-frequency 0DTE seller pays; cost as a share
  of R is what a risk-budget comparison pays.

  [MODELED]  And what the model itself says the credit is at 16 delta
  0.25 DTE, so the $0.05-$0.20 band above is not a straw man: on a
  $770 index at sigma=20%, the 0.25-DTE 16-delta put is at
  $766.02 and the 5-wide wing at $761.02, for a credit of $0.32 per share
  = $32.39 per contract on a $468 R.  A CREDIT THAT SMALL IS THE
  MODEL'S OWN ARTEFACT: the model has no skew, so an OTM put is priced
  at the same vol as the ATM one.  A real 0DTE 16-delta put carries a
  much higher implied vol.  Nothing in this section should be used to
  price a real 0DTE ticket.

  [ESTIMATED -- UNDATED VENDOR MARKETING PAGE]  The widely quoted
  '0DTE = 59% of SPX volume' figure has NO AS-OF DATE.  Per
  `trading-decision-data-catalogue` (SKILL.md line 163 and
  `references/options-positioning-data.md` section 3.1): an undated
  market-share statistic is not a statistic, it can be neither right
  nor wrong, and it must NOT be put in a chart -- with the reason
  stated when asked.  This study therefore reports NO 0DTE
  share-of-volume number at all, dated or not.

13. 0DTE, [ILLUSTRATIVE] ONLY -- and it CANNOT BE EXTRAPOLATED
==============================================================================

This section exists because leaving 0DTE out entirely would hide what
the data CAN and CANNOT say.  It is NOT a result:

  * it enters at the session OPEN and settles at the session CLOSE.
    A real 0DTE trader's P&L is dominated by the INTRADAY PATH -- the
    open, the lunchtime drift, whether the strike was touched at 11:00
    or at 15:55 -- and NONE of that is in these bars;
  * there is no intraday stop, no profit target, no roll, and no
    intraday marking, so the daily high and low are never used;
  * strikes are solved from the OPEN with a vol built from the 3-session
    lagged VIX, which is a ~30-day vol.  A real 0DTE chain is priced off
    an intraday, term-structured surface this study does not have;
  * strikes are unrounded, and at 0DTE the listing grid ($1 on SPY) is
    the same size as the whole modelled move;
  * the model's own 0.25-DTE credit is an artefact of flat-vol pricing.

It is printed once, it is not carried into any verdict, and nothing in
it may be scaled up, annualised or compared with a real 0DTE book.

  [ILLUSTRATIVE]  Even taken at face value this table is a 0DTE

15. WHAT THIS STUDY CANNOT SEE, STATED PLAINLY
==============================================================================

  1. NO TERM STRUCTURE.  One vol -- the 3-session-lagged VIX, a ~30-day
     vol -- prices 7 DTE and 730 DTE alike.  A real 730-DTE option has
     its own implied vol, its own skew and its own dividend assumptions.
     This is the single largest weakness of a study whose subject IS
     tenor, and it is not repairable from these feeds.
  2. NO SKEW SURFACE, and a skew surface is itself a function of tenor.
     Test 5 sizes the direction-ambiguous part of it (+-1.5 vol points
     on the sold legs).  It cannot size the tenor-dependent part.
  3. FIXED DOLLAR WING (N2).  Appendix A is the control; read every
     5-wide number beside it.
  4. NO ASSIGNMENT, NO EARLY EXERCISE, NO DIVIDENDS.  SPY/QQQ/IWM are
     American and physically settled; every leg here is European and
     cash-settled.  This omits an EXERCISE EVENT whose annual
     probability is roughly proportional to the holding period, so a
     730-DTE short call's real risk is understated by far more than a
     7-DTE one's.  The model also pays no dividend, so a 730-DTE
     modelled call is too expensive and a 730-DTE modelled put too
     cheap -- and the covered call's whole economics turn on dividends.
  5. NO INTRADAY PATH, so no 0DTE result and no intra-trade stop.
  6. NO VEGA P&L PATH.  Vol is a function of the lagged VIX close, so a
     position does experience some mark-to-market vol movement -- but
     this is a 3-session-lagged series, not a live surface, and the
     long-tenor cells' vega exposure is therefore not measured.
  7. NO ROLL, NO MARGIN, NO BUYING-POWER CONSTRAINT, NO LEVERAGE LIMIT.
     The 730-DTE cells would in reality tie up margin for two years.
  8. THE ENTRY CALENDAR DIFFERS BY TENOR (N3).  The 7-DTE programme
     enters ~1300 times and the 730-DTE programme ~12 times, at
     different dates.  A tenor difference is a difference in holding
     period AND in which dates were sampled.
  9. ONE WINDOW.  25.6 years, 306 complete months, 24 complete years,
     and two crashes.  That is enough to see a tail, not enough to
     grade every corner -- which is exactly what Test 3 measures.
 10. SEVEN STRUCTURES, PRE-REGISTERED, AND NO OTHERS.  In particular
     nothing here speaks to calendars, diagonals, ratios, jade lizards
     or any 3-leg structure.
