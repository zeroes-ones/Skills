## Key EM Central Banks

| Central Bank | Currency | Meeting Frequency | Typical Rate | Special Considerations |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| People's Bank of China (PBoC) | CNY | Ad-hoc | Via Loan Prime Rate (LPR) monthly | Not single-rate decisions; uses MLF, RRR, LPR levers |
| Central Bank of Turkey (TCMB) | TRY | 12/year (monthly until 2025) | Very high (40-50%) | Political intervention risk. Governors fired for not cutting |
| South African Reserve Bank (SARB) | ZAR | 6/year | Moderate (8-9%) | Credible inflation targeter; rand responds to real yield |
| Banco Central do Brasil (BCB) | BRL | 8/year | High (10-14%) | Very credible; BRL carry trade heavily influenced by Copom |
| Banco de México (Banxico) | MXN | 8/year | High (9-12%) | Often follows Fed with lag; MXN sensitive to US-MX rate diff |
| Bank of Korea (BoK) | KRW | 8/year | Moderate (3-4%) | Export-sensitive; KRW moves on growth outlook more than rates |
| Reserve Bank of India (RBI) | INR | 6/year | Moderate (6-7%) | Managed float; RBI intervenes heavily to manage INR |
| Bank Indonesia (BI) | IDR | 12/year | High (5-7%) | Heavy intervention; IDR stability is primary mandate |

## Pre-Meeting Checklist

For EACH upcoming central bank meeting within 2 weeks:
- [ ] Current rate: [VERIFY from central bank website, NOT training data]
- [ ] Market pricing: OIS-implied probability of hike/hold/cut [VERIFY from Bloomberg/Reuters]
- [ ] Economist consensus: Survey median and range [VERIFY]
- [ ] Recent data: Inflation, employment, GDP since last meeting
- [ ] Speeches since last meeting: Hawkish/dovish leaning tally
- [ ] Surprise potential: Gap between market pricing and economist consensus

## Trading Rules Around CB Meetings

1. **No carry trades held through meetings.** Close 24h before.
2. **Reduce all positions by 50%** in the affected currency 4h before.
3. **Do not trade the first 15 minutes** after release. Let the algos fight.
4. **Wait for the press conference** (ECB, Fed, BoE, RBA) before direction trades. The statement is only half the story.
5. **Rate decision ≠ currency direction.** Rate cuts can be bullish if "dovish cut" was priced in and the statement is hawkish.
