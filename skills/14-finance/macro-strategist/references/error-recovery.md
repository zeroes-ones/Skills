# Macro Strategist Error Recovery

## Additional Error Patterns

### 1. Data Revision Risk
**Symptom**: Strategy based on GDP data that was revised significantly in subsequent releases
**Root Cause**: Advance GDP estimates have average revision of ±0.6% (absolute). Q1 2024 advance was 1.6% (annualized); final was 1.3%.
**Fix**: Weight GDP Now (Atlanta Fed GDPNow, NY Fed Nowcast) as much as advance GDP. Use multiple nowcasts. Don't trade on advance GDP alone.
**Lesson**: The "first" GDP print is an estimate of an estimate. The true number emerges months later. Trade the trend, not the point.

### 2. Model Uncertainty in r-star Estimation
**Symptom**: Position sized based on "Fed is 150bps above r-star" but r-star estimate was wrong
**Root Cause**: r-star estimates range from 0.5% (LW model) to 2.5% (adjusted for fiscal deficits). The Fed itself publishes a range (0.5-1.5% in latest SEP).
**Fix**: Use r-star as a DIRECTIONAL concept (restrictive vs accommodative), not a precise level. If r-star estimates cluster, use the median. Don't trade the exact gap.
**Lesson**: r-star is unobservable. The range of credible estimates is 100-200bps wide. That's not an edge — it's noise.

### 3. Narrative Fallacy
**Symptom**: Constructed a perfect macro narrative; market did the opposite
**Root Cause**: Narratives are backward-looking rationalizations. The market prices forward-looking probability distributions. Your story connects dots that the market already discounted.
**Fix**: Separate analysis from narrative. Analysis = "growth is X, inflation is Y, policy is Z." Narrative = "and therefore..." The "and therefore" is the dangerous part. Present the analysis; let the positioning follow from process, not story.
**Lesson**: The most dangerous macro call is the one with the best story. Stories feel true regardless of their probability.

### 4. Black Swan Blindness
**Symptom**: Scenario analysis covered the "likely" outcomes but missed the pandemic/sanctions/war event
**Root Cause**: Scenario design anchored to recent history. 2019 scenarios didn't include "global pandemic"; 2021 scenarios didn't include "Russia invades Ukraine."
**Fix**: Always include one "unmodelable" scenario with generic risk-off parameters: -30% equities, +20% vol, -10% risky assets. You can't predict the trigger, but you can predict the market response pattern.
**Lesson**: The next crisis will not look like the last crisis. Prepare for the response pattern, not the trigger.
