# Currency War & Devaluation

## Competitive Devaluation Dynamics
A currency war occurs when multiple countries simultaneously weaken their currencies to gain export advantage. Since exchange rates are zero-sum (one up = another down), not all countries can weaken simultaneously.

### Historical Currency War Episodes
| Period | Trigger | Outcome |
|--------|---------|---------|
| 1930s | Depression-era competitive devaluations (beggar-thy-neighbor) | Trade collapse, eventually Bretton Woods system |
| 1985 Plaza Accord | Dollar too strong; G5 coordinated to weaken USD | Dollar fell 40% vs yen, 50% vs D-mark |
| 2010-2011 | Post-GFC: "Currency war" rhetoric as EM protested Fed QE | EM capital controls, FX intervention |
| 2022 | BOJ intervenes to support yen; China manages CNY weaker | Mixed results; fundamental flows dominate |

## Currency Intervention Mechanics
### Sterilized Intervention
Central bank buys/sells FX, offsets monetary impact with domestic open market operations.

### Unsterilized Intervention
Central bank buys/sells FX, allowing monetary base to change. More powerful but changes domestic monetary conditions.

## Capital Controls
| Type | Mechanism | Examples |
|------|-----------|----------|
| Inflow controls | Tax on foreign investment, minimum holding periods | Brazil (2010), Thailand (2006) |
| Outflow controls | Limits on capital repatriation, FX conversion caps | China (ongoing), Malaysia (1998) |
| Currency peg defense | Trading band, direct intervention, interest rate hikes | Hong Kong (USD peg), Switzerland (2011-2015) |

## Dedollarization Trend
- BRICS expansion → alternative reserve currency push
- Bilateral trade in local currencies (CNY-RUB, INR-AED)
- Gold accumulation by central banks (record purchases 2022-2024)
- IMF COFER data: USD share declining from ~71% (2000) to ~58% (2024)
