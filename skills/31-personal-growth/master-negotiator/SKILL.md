## Quickstart (30s)
1. State your BATNA in one sentence.
2. Write your reservation price and ideal target.
3. Prepare one MESO (package offer with 2 options).
4. Schedule the negotiation with an agenda and timebox.

## Ground Rules
- Mechanical triggers: before the negotiation, do a 10-minute calm breathing and repeat your objective.
- Negative constraints: never accept verbal-only commitments without written confirmation for material terms.
- Ethical boundary: do not knowingly misrepresent facts or fabricate alternatives.
- Transparency: document offers and record time-stamped notes during negotiation.

## Core Workflow
STANDARD: Preparation (60–120 minutes)
1. Define objective spectrum: ideal, target, reservation (walk-away), and BATNA summary.
2. Research market benchmarks, counterpart constraints, and decision-makers.
3. List tradeable levers (compensation, timing, scope, IP, advisory roles, equity, onboarding terms).
4. Design 2–3 MESOs: package offers each with a priced equivalent and clear pros/cons.
5. Script 2 opening lines and a set of clarifying questions.

STANDARD: Opening & Anchoring
1. If making first offer, anchor high with data and rationale; use ranges when uncertainty is high.
2. Present value frame: tie the ask to the problem you solve and the outcomes you enable.
3. After you anchor, pause and be comfortable with silence — let counterpart respond first.

DEEP: Concession Strategy <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Logrolling: identify items you can give that cost you little but the counterpart values highly; use them to trade for high-value asks.
2. Concession pacing: start with smaller concessions and escalate only if reciprocity is explicit and measurable.
3. Use conditional concessions: "If you do X, then I can reduce my ask by Y" — keep exchange symmetry.
4. Failure narrative: a startup founder bundled many concessions to close quickly and later regretted recurring operational costs; the post-audit revealed missing cost models. Fix: include future cost analysis when conceding ongoing obligations.
5. Exercise: create a concession ladder for your negotiation, listing 6 concessions in descending value — expected output: ready-to-use concession script.

DEEP: Emotional & Cross-Cultural (10+min)
1. Emotional labeling: name emotions when they surface ("I hear concern about timing") — defuses escalation.
2. Cultural calibration: research counterpart norms (decision speed, face-saving requirements, evidence preferences).
3. Use pace and tone to match counterpart; if uncertainty, err on the side of respectful deference.
4. War story: during a cross-border deal, direct pricing offended one side; pivoting to problem-solution framing removed friction and preserved relationships.
5. Edge case: power-imbalanced negotiations — secure written commitments, consider escrow, involve neutral third-party if needed.

DEEP: Post-Negotiation Audit (10+min)
1. Immediately after, capture: final terms, concessions given, concessions received, phrases that worked, failed tactics, and emotions observed.
2. Identify 3 wins and 3 missed opportunities; for each missed opportunity, write an improvement action.
3. Update negotiation templates and BATNA assumptions.
4. War story: a salesperson who did routine post-audits improved win rate by 14% in 6 months via pattern fixes.

## Expanded Error Decoder (5-8 rows)
| Error Message / Pitfall | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|-------------------------|------------|-----|--------|
| "We gave too much away" | No concession framework; urgency bias | Use MESOs and conditional concessions; add decision guardrails | Rush costs value |
| "Deal collapsed last minute" | No interim commitment; misaligned incentives | Use signed term-sheet, deposit, or clearly tied milestones | Formally lock major commitments early |
| "Counter anchored unbelievably low" | Poor benchmarking, weak BATNA | Re-anchor with data and show alternative offers; strengthen BATNA externally | Preparation is leverage |
| "Cultural misread" | Ignoring norms, tone, face issues | Slow down, ask about priorities, and adapt behavior | Cultural humility protects deals |
| "Scope creep after close" | Vague language and missing definitions | Add precise definitions, acceptance criteria, and change mechanisms | Define scope tightly & include change process |
| "Emotional escalation" | Identity threat, disrespect | Use labeling, cooling time, and reconvene with agenda | Emotions are negotiable signals, not facts |

## Best Practices (8-10)
1. Prepare BATNA, reservation, and target in writing; rehearse them aloud.
2. Use MESOs proactively — create at least one package with non-salary components.
3. Always require a written confirmation for material items before performing work.
4. Keep a concessions log that records date, item, value, and reciprocity condition.
5. Use silence and calibrated questions as control tools ("How did you arrive at that number?").
6. Run a 10-minute role-play with a trusted partner; record and review for tone and clarity.
7. For cross-cultural deals, prepare a cultural checklist: decision-makers, pace, formality, and hierarchy.
8. After closure, schedule a 48-hour post-audit to capture lessons while fresh.
9. Avoid "split the difference" unless an objective benchmark justifies it.
10. Always document next steps and deadlines to prevent ambiguity.

## Metrics & Measurement (concrete)
- Outcome vs Target: percent of negotiations closed at or above target vs baseline.
- Concession Efficiency: ratio of value gained to value given (higher is better).
- Time-to-close: average negotiation duration; track median and outliers.
- Rework rate: % of deals requiring amendment within 6 months.
- Calibration improvement: reduction in error between estimated BATNA and realized alternatives over time.
