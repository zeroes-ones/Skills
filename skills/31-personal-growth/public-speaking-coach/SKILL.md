3. Identify 3 core takeaways.
4. Record a 60-second practice and review one improvement.

## Ground Rules
- Mechanical triggers: always open with a clear hook and set expectations.
- Negative constraints: do not clutter slides with bullet-dense text.
- Safety: prepare a 30-second fallback if AV fails (talk without slides).
- Rehearsal cadence: at least 2 recorded rehearsals and one live test with an audience for talks >15 minutes.

## Core Workflow
STANDARD: Structure & Story
1. Choose a storytelling framework: Hero's Journey for personal narrative, Pixar spine for arc, or Assertion-Evidence for technical talks.
2. Build talk as: Hook -> Problem -> Insight -> Evidence -> Call to action. Keep sequence logical and repeat takeaways.
3. Limit core takeaways to 3 and weave them through openings, transitions, and closing.

STANDARD: Slides & Visuals
1. Use assertion-evidence slides: one assertion + one visual per slide; avoid dense text.
2. Use typography and color for legibility; test slides at thumbnail size.
3. Keep slides to support narrative — each slide should communicate one idea in 3 seconds.

DEEP: Presence & Delivery <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Vocal variety: practice deliberate pitch, pace, and pausing. Mark breathing and pause points in script.
2. Movement and gestures: design purposeful movement anchored to sections (enter, highlight, transition, close).
3. Manage physicality: use posture, weight shifts, and grounded stance to convey presence.
4. War story: a speaker who relied on slides lost audience when slides failed; rehearsing a slide-free delivery recovered authority and led to stronger Q&A.
5. Edge case: presenters with accents — practice enunciation, slow slight tempo, and audience check-ins to ensure comprehension.
6. Exercise: record a 10-minute section focusing only on vocal variety and review for monotony and filler words; expected output: 3 targeted corrections.

DEEP: Q&A & Handling Anxiety <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Prepare answers to likely questions and craft bridging phrases to core takeaways.
2. Use techniques: repeat the question, clarify, answer briefly, and bridge back with a takeaway.
3. Handling anxiety: breathwork (box breathing), brief exposure (mini-rehearsals), and pre-performance rituals.
4. War story: a TEDx speaker reduced stage anxiety by simulating an audience of 5 at home and progressively increased audience size in rehearsals.
5. Exercise: practice 6 anticipated Q&As with a partner; expected outcome: concise answers and two bridging sentences.

DEEP: Virtual Mastery <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Camera framing: place camera at eye-line, frame chest-up, and practice looking at camera for moments of eye contact.
2. Engagement: use chat prompts, polls, and short interactive activities to maintain attention every 7–12 minutes.
3. Technical redundancy: wired Ethernet, backup laptop, local slide copy, and phone hotspot available.
4. Edge case: low-bandwidth audiences — prepare low-data slide variants and offline handouts.
5. Exercise: run a 30-min mock virtual session with recorded chat interactions; expected output: playback review of engagement opportunities.

## Expanded Error Decoder (5-8 rows)
| Error Message / Pitfall | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|-------------------------|------------|-----|--------|
| "Slides distracted audience" | Visual overload, reading slides | Strip to assertion + visual; rehearse without slides | Slides are supports, not scripts |
| "I blanked on stage" | Memorization dependence, anxiety | Use anchor points, practice transitions, and rehearsal variations | Internalize structure over verbatim text |
| "Audience disengaged" | Weak hook or irrelevant content | Rework hook and contextualize takeaways to audience needs | Attention must be earned early |
| "Q&A derails talk" | Poor framing and unprepared answers | Bridge answers to takeaways and set Q&A rules | Q&A is an opportunity to reinforce messages |
| "Virtual tech failure" | No redundancy or test | Prepare fallback plan: continue without slides; share slide PDF after | Test tech and have graceful failure modes |
| "Slide text too small" | Design oversight | Test at thumbnail size and on phone | Legibility is non-negotiable

## Best Practices (8-10 items)
1. Start with a strong throughline and three takeaways; anchor every section to them.
2. Rehearse aloud and record at least twice, reviewing for filler words and pacing.
3. Use assertion-evidence slides and keep text minimal (6 words preferred).
4. Prepare a slide-free 3-minute fallback opener in case of AV failure.
5. Use polls or chat to re-engage virtual audiences at least once every 10 minutes.
6. Mark pauses explicitly in script for emphasis; practice the silence.
7. For technical talks, include a worked example or demo and pre-warn the audience of complexity.
8. Always collect one metric post-talk (survey, signup, or action) to measure impact.
9. Run a 48-hour post-audit capturing 3 what-worked and 3 improvements.
10. Use a consistent rehearsal environment similar to the live setting when possible.

## Metrics & Measurement (concrete)
- Takeaway clarity: >70% audience correctly list 1–3 takeaways on post-talk survey.
- Engagement: average watch time (virtual) or applause/participation indicators (live).
- Rehearsal improvement: reduction in filler words by 30% over rehearsals.
- Conversion: at least one audience action (signup/intro/request) within 7 days.
