## Quickstart (30s)
1. Sit for 3 minutes: focus on breath, count inhales to 10 and back.
2. On distraction, note "thinking" and return to breath.
3. After practice, write one sentence: "I noticed..."
4. Repeat daily at same time for 7 days.

<!-- STANDARD: 3min -->
## Ground Rules
- Mechanical triggers: practice at the same time daily to build habit.
- Negative constraints: avoid forcing insights; practice is observation-first.
- Safety: if difficult emotions arise, seek teacher support and pause practice if overwhelmed.
- Ethical: do not use this skill as substitute for clinical therapy if trauma is present; refer to professionals.

## Core Workflow
STANDARD: Focused Attention
1. Sit upright, set a timer for 10–20 minutes.
2. Anchor on breath sensations; when distracted, label and return.
3. Log one observation after session (insight, emotion, body sensation).

STANDARD: Open Monitoring & Metta
1. Open monitoring: widen attention to sounds, sensations, and thoughts without engagement.
2. Metta: cultivate loving-kindness with phrases for self and others (may you be happy, may you be safe).
3. Body-scan: practice 10–30 minute body scans to rebuild body awareness and reduce dissociation.

DEEP: Contemplative Integration <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Mindful eating: slow down a meal for 10 minutes, note textures, urges, and feelings. Repeat across 3 meals and compare notes; look for patterns in craving triggers and sensory dulling.
2. Mindful listening: during a 5-minute conversation, keep a tally of when you are planning a response vs listening; aim to reduce planning time by 25% over 4 weeks.
3. Micro-retreat design: schedule a 1-day silent or semi-silent day with alternating 30–45 minute sits, 20–30 minute walking, and 30–45 minute journaling blocks. Debrief with a teacher or peer.
4. War story: an engineering manager added a 5-minute mindful pause before his 3 daily touchpoints. After 8 weeks they observed a 40% reduction in reactive Slack messages and reported fewer escalations during sprints — the team noticed clearer leadership tone.
5. Failure narrative: a participant did long sits sporadically without integration and experienced short-term calm but no behavioral change; the missing link was integration rituals (micro-pauses and community accountability).
6. Edge case: for shift workers and parents, long sits are impractical — design a 'snack' practice (3 breaths, 30 seconds) tied to routine events (before bottle-feeding, after commute) to create micro-continuity.
7. Exercises:
   a. 7-day mindful eating log: for 7 consecutive days, pick one meal, eat slowly, and record three observations each time. Outcome: increased sensory awareness and ~10% reduction in mindless snacking.
   b. Listening challenge: in one week, track 5 conversations and summarize each in one sentence after listening — expected outcome: improved recall and empathy cues.
8. Reflection prompts: "When did I notice autopilot today?" "What triggered reactivity?" Write answers in journal and revisit weekly to detect patterns.
9. Integrate technology: set phone reminder titled "Pause" three times daily and use it as a prompt for 30 seconds breath anchoring.
10. Coaching note: when working with clients who have high cognitive load, emphasize micro-practices and anchor them to existing habits rather than creating new time slots.

DEEP: Retreat Design & Integration <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Retreat scaffolding: choose a schedule template (e.g., 45/30/45 sits/walking/journal) and adapt to personal limits. Use progressive exposure: try 1-day, then 3-day, then 5–7 day retreats.
2. Device policy nuances: full-device-off, flight-mode-only, or scheduled check-ins — pick based on responsibility load (caregivers may need scheduled contact windows).
3. Post-retreat integration plan: map 3 daily anchors (morning breath, midday pause, evening reflection) and a weekly community debrief for 6–12 weeks.
4. Failure narrative: skipping post-retreat integration leads to 'rosy retreat effect' — intense insight during retreat followed by quick reversion. Mitigate by assigning accountability partners and concrete daily anchors before the retreat ends.
5. Edge case: returning to high-stress jobs — create a 'decompression hour' (40 minutes) with breathwork + walking for first week back.
6. Exercise: design a 3-day weekend retreat for yourself with clear timings, teaching resources (podcasts, talks), and an integration partner. Run a pilot 1-day variation and iterate.
7. Logistics checklist: sleep schedule aligned with practice blocks, simple whole-food meals, comfortable seating/cushions, emergency contact list, and clear expectations for others in household.
8. Measurement: after retreat, use a weekly 4-question self-check for 8 weeks: sleep, reactivity, presence moments, and compassion acts — track changes and adjust anchors.
9. War story: a teacher who required students to publish a 4-week integration plan kept 80% of attendees practicing at 4-week follow-up versus 40% without such a plan.
10. Coaching note: schedule a 1:1 check-in two weeks after retreat to solve real-world reintegration obstacles.

DEEP: Compassion Cultivation <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Progressive metta sequencing: begin with self-directed phrases for 1 minute, then extend to loved ones, neutral persons, difficult persons, and finally all beings — 3–5 minutes per stage across weeks.
2. Compassion journaling protocol: after a triggering event, write 3 compassionate responses you would say to a friend; then write them to yourself and re-read daily for three days.
3. Failure narrative: overextending compassion practices without boundaries can produce burnout in caregivers — mitigate with supervision and scheduled rest.
4. Edge case: practitioners with high self-criticism may resist metta — start with objective observations about the body or breath and slowly introduce warm phrases anchored to small wins.
5. Exercises:
   a. 14-day self-compassion streak: 5 minutes daily metta for self; log mood before and after each session. Expected outcome: reduction in negative self-talk frequency.
   b. Difficult-person sequence: once per week, perform a 10-minute metta practice directed toward a difficult person and journal shifts in perspective; expected outcome: increased cognitive flexibility around conflict cues.
6. War story: in a six-week compassion course, participants who paired metta with brief acts of kindness reported higher sustained practice rates and greater prosocial behavior than those who used metta alone.
7. Research note: compassion cultivation training (CCT) has RCT evidence showing reductions in depression and increases in social connection; use program modules as templates for structured practice.
8. Coaching note: combine compassion practices with behavioral activation (small help tasks) to anchor compassion in behavior.

## Expanded Error Decoder (8 rows)
| Error Message / Pitfall | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|-------------------------|------------|-----|--------|
| "I can't stop thinking" | Normal attention wandering | Note the thought and return to breath; normalize wandering | Wandering is part of the practice |
| "Practice triggered strong emotion" | Trauma or unresolved content surfaced | Pause practice and seek teacher/therapist support | Practice can surface material that needs care |
| "No measurable benefit" | Inconsistent practice or wrong technique | Commit to 8 weeks with a simple log and small daily target | Benefits accumulate with consistency |
| "I feel detached or numb" | Overpractice or dissociation | Shorten sessions, increase grounding (body-scan), and consult teacher | Balance noticing with grounding activities |
| "Community mismatch" | Wrong sangha or teacher fit | Try different communities and teachers until resonance found | Cultural/context fit matters for retention |
| "Perfection trap" | Trying to do practice 'perfectly' | Normalize variability, focus on frequency over ideal sessions | Aim for consistency, not perfection |
| "Compassion fatigue" | Over-identification and overgiving | Set boundaries, self-care routines, and professional support | Compassion requires capacity management |
| "Slip-back after retreat" | No integration plan | Implement daily anchors and accountability partners | Integration is essential to sustain gains |

## Best Practices (expanded 12)
1. Start with 10 minutes daily and increase by 5 minutes every 2–4 weeks as comfortable.
2. Keep a simple practice log (date, duration, practice type, one-line observation) and review weekly.
3. Combine focused attention with at least one compassion practice weekly and a body-scan fortnightly.
4. Use micro-practices (1–5 minutes) during busy days to maintain continuity and avoid all-or-nothing thinking.
5. Establish at least one integration anchor (breath before meetings) and a social accountability check (weekly buddy).
6. When difficult emotions arise, pause practice and consult a qualified teacher or clinician rather than pushing through alone.
7. Test a 1-day micro-retreat before committing to longer retreats; iterate structure based on fatigue and insight.
8. Create an explicit post-retreat integration plan with daily anchors, community check-ins, and a 2-week coach follow-up.
9. Use guided meditations and high-quality programs (MBSR, CCT) early on; transition to silent practice as skill matures.
10. Respect cultural and personal boundaries — adapt language and metaphors to the practitioner's background.
11. Track both process (minutes practiced, anchors used) and outcome metrics (reactivity, sleep) to inform adjustments.
12. Prioritize safety: have a crisis plan and therapist referral list before intensifying practice.

## Metrics & Measurement (concrete + template)
- Practice adherence: % of days with practice (target >=70% over 8 weeks). Template: weekly adherence = practiced_days / 7.
- Subjective reactivity: weekly self-rated reactivity scale (1–10) decrease of 1 point over 8 weeks. Template: record pre-week avg and post-week avg.
- Sleep quality: nights with restful sleep increase (%) using simple sleep question: "Did you feel rested? (y/n)" tracked weekly.
- Presence metrics: number of mindful moments (intentional pauses) per workday (target >=3). Track using tally column in weekly log.
- Compassion metric: frequency of deliberate compassionate acts per week. Track with checkboxes in log.
- Decision-support dashboard: simple spreadsheet with columns: date, minutes_practiced, practice_type, anchors_used(0/1), reactivity_score, sleep_rest(0/1), compassion_acts, notes. Analyze 4-week rolling averages.

## Exercises & Templates (expanded)
Exercise 1 — 5-minute breath anchor
1. Sit or stand, close eyes or lower gaze, breathe 6–8 breaths slowly.
2. Count the inhale to 4 and exhale to 6, notice sensations, and write one sentence after.
Expected outcome: short-term calm and one observation.

Exercise 2 — 10-minute body scan
1. Lie down or sit. Move attention slowly from toes to head, noting sensations without judgment.
2. After, write one observation about tension release.
Expected outcome: increased body-awareness and a small reduction in tension.

Exercise 3 — 7-day mindful eating (template)
1. Choose one meal daily for 7 days. Eat slowly for first 5–10 minutes and note: textures, urge to check phone, and fullness cue.
2. Log three observations each day.
Expected outcome: improved satiety cues and reduced mindless snacking.

Exercise 4 — 14-day self-compassion streak
1. 5 minutes daily metta for self for 14 days; record mood before and after.
2. At day 14, compare frequency of negative self-talk to baseline.
Expected outcome: measurable decrease in negative self-talk frequency.
