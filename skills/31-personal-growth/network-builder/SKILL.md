## Quickstart (30s)
1. Export 50 recent contacts to CSV.
2. Tag top 10 priority contacts for re-engagement.
3. Send three personalized 2-line messages adding value.
4. Log responses in CRM.

## Ground Rules
- Mechanical triggers: after every meeting, write one line: "How I can help" and schedule follow-up.
- Negative constraints: avoid transactional asks in first outreach; lead with value.
- Privacy: store only contact metadata you have permission to use.
- Consent: when saving notes about discussions, avoid sensitive personal data.

## Core Workflow
STANDARD: CRM & Categorization
1. Export contacts and categorize by strength: strong, weak, dormant, target.
2. Assign a 12-week cadence for each category with action templates.
3. Maintain a one-line "how I can help" and a "last value offered" field.

STANDARD: Weak Tie Activation
1. Identify dormant or weak ties with potential overlap to goals.
2. Draft personalized re-engagement message offering specific value (article, intro, feedback).
3. If positive, schedule a 20–30 minute 1:1 and follow-up with a concrete next step.

DEEP: Conference Strategy <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Pre-select 8 targets to meet and research them for conversation triggers, mutual contacts, and recent work.
2. Pre-outreach with a concise hook and propose a 15-min slot, offering mutual value (e.g., read a draft, share intro).
3. On-site: prioritize depth over quantity — aim for 3 high-quality meetings, have an instant note after each meeting.
4. Post-conference follow-up within 48 hours: personalized message, resource promised, and scheduling next step.
5. War story: an attendee used pre-event briefs to secure two intro meetings that led to advisory roles within three months.
6. Edge case: virtual conferences — use meeting scheduling slots and pre-shared collateral to stand out.
7. Exercise: for your next event, pre-outreach 5 targets with a draft hook and measure response rate; expected outcome: 1–2 scheduled 1:1s.

DEEP: Community & Mastermind Design <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Define clear purpose, intake criteria, cadence (monthly/biweekly), and contribution expectations.
2. Design onboarding: member card, expectations, and a first-week value offer.
3. Structure sessions: check-in, hot-seat, resource swap, and action commitments.
4. Measure contributions and outcomes quarterly (introductions, projects started, jobs filled).
5. Failure narrative: a mastermind with low structure devolved into socializing; restructuring with a facilitator and rules restored productivity.
6. Exercise: draft a one-page mastermind charter and a first-session agenda; expected outcome: clear pilot plan.

DEEP: Networking for Introverts <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Focus on 1:1 pre-arranged connections with clear outcomes and time limits.
2. Prepare deep questions that move beyond surface-level small talk (e.g., "What's a decision you're proud of this year?").
3. Use asynchronous value exchanges (short notes, curated resources) to maintain relationships without constant social energy.
4. War story: an introvert found success by hosting small micro-dinners and following up with tailored resources — slower but deeper network growth.
5. Edge case: social anxiety — create a safety plan and a co-host to share burden.
6. Exercise: prepare three deep questions and use them in the next 1:1; expected outcome: deeper connection and one actionable follow-up.

## Expanded Error Decoder (5-8 rows)
| Error Message / Pitfall | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|-------------------------|------------|-----|--------|
| "No replies" | Generic, mass outreach without value | Personalize with clear value and context; reference mutual connections | Specific, tailored asks produce replies |
| "I feel transactional" | Asking for favors too early | Lead with value, offer help first, and build rapport | Reciprocity works long-term |
| "Contacts vanish" | No follow-up or inconsistent cadence | Schedule automated reminders and personal follow-ups | Discipline outlasts goodwill |
| "Burnout from events" | Overcommitting to large events | Limit to 1–2 events per quarter, build recovery windows | Sustainable networking is paced |
| "Mastermind stagnation" | Poor structure or commitment | Reintroduce facilitation, explicit actions and accountability | Structure scales quality

## Best Practices (8-10)
1. Maintain a one-line value note for each contact to guide future outreach.
2. Limit active nurture set to ~50 prioritized contacts to maintain depth.
3. Personalize the first sentence of any outreach with a specific trigger or reference.
4. Always follow up within 48 hours after meetings with a clear next step.
5. Offer introductions proactively and track outcomes to maintain reciprocity records.
6. Convert low-value recurring meetings to async updates when possible.
7. Use event debriefs (15 minutes) immediately after events to capture connections while fresh.
8. Test a 12-week cadence — engage, add value, schedule a touchpoint, and measure outcomes.
9. For introverts, favor small curated gatherings and written value exchanges over mass networking.
10. Use CRM tags for intent (mentor, hire, collaborate) and prioritize accordingly.

## Metrics & Measurement (concrete)
- Meaningful reconnections: number of 1:1s with substantive outcomes (target >=6 per 12 weeks).
- Value events: number of introductions, gigs, mentorships initiated via network (target >=3 per 12 weeks).
- Response rate: replies to warm outreach (target >=20%).
- Reciprocity score: ratio of value given to value received (aim for >1 over time).
- Community engagement: active participation rate in mastermind or community (>50%).
