# Life Architect
Portability: personal-growth / cross-platform

<!-- QUICK: 30s -->
One-line: A repeatable life-design protocol to discover purpose, craft a 3–5 year vision, and translate it into cascading plans and measurable life-satisfaction indicators.

| Code | Requirement |
|------|-------------|
| RP1  | A quiet 90-minute slot for reflection |
| RP2  | Access to calendar and last 12 months of time logs |
| RP3  | Basic spreadsheet or note tool (Notion/Sheets) |
| RP4  | Willingness to write candidly about failures and wins |
| RP5  | Access to income/expense summary for financial portfolio view |
| RP6  | Up to 3 trusted mentors for feedback loop |
| RP7  | Ability to run a short survey with close relations (optional) |

| Loop | Purpose | Input | Output |
|------|---------|-------|--------|
| 0 | Baseline discovery | 90-min reflection, calendar, time log | Draft Ikigai, baseline metrics |
| 1 | Vision drafting | Drafts, values list, energy audit | 3-5 year written vision, anti-vision |
| 2 | Goal cascading | Vision, annual goals template | Annual + quarterly goals, KPIs |
| 3 | Validation & cadence | Mentor feedback, reality check | Final plan + weekly rhythm, metrics dashboard |

## Quickstart (30s -> tangible)
1. Open a blank note titled "Life Architect — YEAR".
2. Write one sentence for each quadrant: Passion, Mission, Vocation, Profession.
3. Scan your calendar for the past 3 months and note 3 biggest energy drains.
4. Draft one 3-year vision sentence.
5. Commit a single weekly action that maps to the vision.

## Ground Rules
- Mechanical triggers: perform an energy audit before annual planning; block a 2-hour vision session every year.
- Negative constraints: avoid optimization for external short-term metrics (likes, prestige) during vision drafting.
- Confidentiality: treat drafted anti-visions and regrets as private working notes.
- Scope: this skill is for personal life-design, not job placement or clinical therapy.

## Core Workflow
STANDARD: Setup (60–120 minutes)
1. Gather data: calendar (last 12 months), expense snapshot, health markers, recent feedback, 3 most meaningful accomplishments and failures.
2. Run Ikigai intersection: list top 8 passions, 8 skills, 8 market needs, 8 monetizable expressions. Highlight 2–4 overlap candidates.
3. Values clarification: pick 12 possible values, rank them, then choose top 6 and write 1-line reason for each.

STANDARD: Vision Crafting (90 minutes)
1. Write a 3–5 year "Ideal Day" paragraph — include where you are, who you spend time with, the work you do, and 3 numerical KPIs (income range, hours/week on creative work, travel days/year).
2. Draft anti-vision: list 6 signs you'd have "failed" your values (e.g., "if I prioritize prestige over generosity").
3. Create an Odyssey Plan: three 5-year scenarios (Conservative, Growth, Moonshot). For each, list 6 assumptions, first-year milestones, and 3 leading indicators.

STANDARD: Goal Cascading (90 minutes)
1. From chosen Odyssey, derive 3 annual pillars (e.g., Health, Creative Work, Family Capacity).
2. For each pillar, define 3 quarterly outcomes (SMART) and 6 weekly actions that are the smallest repeatable steps to move the quarterly outcomes.
3. Build success metrics: assign a primary KPI and two supporting KPIs for each pillar.

DEEP: Life Portfolio Design <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Map portfolio axes: Career, Health, Relationships, Growth, Finances, Contribution. For each axis, list current allocation (hrs/week, $/month, % attention).
2. Allocate aspirational targets: suggest percentage-based targets (e.g., Career 35%, Health 15%, Relationships 20%, Growth 15%, Finances 10%, Contribution 5%) and justify trade-offs using your values.
3. War story: a client reallocated 12% of their weekly hours from low-ROI meetings into creative studio time; within 6 months they shipped a product that returned 3x their foregone income, validating the portfolio shift.
4. Edge case: dual-career households — map joint portfolio across partners, use shared dashboards, and explicitly allocate individual vs shared time blocks.
5. Exercise: pick one axis and reduce its time by 10% for 6 weeks — log the effects; expected output: list of activities to stop and 3 freed-up hours/week for experimentation.

DEEP: Energy Audit & Habit Mapping <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. Run a two-week time-and-energy log with columns: start, end, activity, energy_before (1–5), energy_after (1–5), satisfaction (1–5), axis tag.
2. Compute Energy ROI = (satisfaction + energy_after - energy_before) / duration_hours. Rank activities by ROI.
3. Failure narrative: someone who ignored low ROI signals kept volunteering for committees for reputation; burnout followed. The pivot: creating a 6-month exit plan and delegating tasks saved emotional bandwidth and improved creative output.
4. Edge case: caretaking responsibilities skew ROI; handle by creating micro-buffers and negotiating external supports.
5. Exercise: run the ROI calc for last week and pick one low-ROI activity to remove; expected outcome: 1–2 freed hours and a retrofit plan for replacement activity.

DEEP: Values & Tradeoff Protocol <!-- DEEP: 10+min -->
1. For each top value, write a ledger of "evidence" in the last year (3 actions that supported the value, 3 that violated it).
2. Failure narrative: a leader whose stated value was "family" but who logged zero family rituals; the mismatch eroded trust at home. The fix: commit to 2 protected family rituals and a transparency conversation.
3. Exercise: run the ledger quickly for one value and schedule 2 corrective actions this week.

## Expanded Error Decoder
| Error Message / Pitfall | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|-------------------------|------------|-----|--------|
| "This vision feels fake" | Rushed drafting, social-comparison, not first-person | Sleep on it, run values ledger, revise in private | Authenticity needs distance and private reflection |
| "No time for plans" | Calendar dominated by others' priorities | Execute 2-week audit, block 4 hours for planning, decline low-ROI recurring events | Scheduling protects planning time |
| "Goals don’t move" | Lack of rituals or unclear next actions | Create weekly ritual with 3 tiny actions and publish accountability | Clarity + rhythm beats motivation |
| "Portfolio reallocation failed" | Underestimated friction & commitments | Add buffer runway, pilot smaller reallocations, delegate tasks | Test small before big reallocations |
| "Energy audit inconclusive" | Poor labeling and inconsistent logs | Standardize tags and run a stricter 7-day audit with check-ins | Data quality matters more than quantity |
| "Partner misalignment" | No shared values conversation | Run partner workshop: values alignment, expectations, time-budget | Shared plans reduce conflict and duplication |
| "Over-optimization to metrics" | Chasing vanity KPIs (likes, awards) | Replace with values-tied KPIs; re-run anti-vision check | Metrics must map to meaning, not signal

## Best Practices (8-10 concrete rules)
1. Start with constraints: choose 3 non-negotiables (e.g., family dinner, weekend recharge) before designing aspirational plans.
2. Prefer measurable leading indicators over lagging vanity metrics (e.g., weekly hours on craft vs social media likes).
3. Keep an anti-vision document to cross-check seductive but misaligned ideas.
4. Use time-budgeting (percent allocation) for portfolios — review quarterly and adjust by ±5% max per quarter unless runway exists.
5. Run a two-week energy audit at least twice a year; schedule it at the slowest business quarter for less noise.
6. When reallocating time, use a 6–12 week pilot with pre-defined success criteria and a rollback plan.
7. Document assumptions in your Odyssey Plans and assign one metric to test each assumption in year one.
8. Publish a one-page plan to a trusted mentor and request a 30-minute review; external critique speeds up blind-spot discovery.
9. Protect planning blocks by setting calendar recurrence and treating them like non-cancellable meetings.
10. Use simplicity: favor one primary goal per pillar to avoid diffusion of effort.

## Metrics & Measurement (concrete framework)
- Time allocation: track hours/week per axis; target variance < ±5% from planned allocation.
- Energy ROI: weekly average ROI > baseline by 10% within 12 weeks after changes.
- Goal progress: % completion of quarterly outcomes — target 60% or better by quarter-end.
- Ritual adherence: weekly ritual completion rate >= 75% over rolling 8-week window.
- Satisfaction delta: Monthly life-satisfaction score (1–10) increase >= 0.5 over 6 months.

## Exercises & Templates
Exercise 1 — 15-minute Ikigai sketch
1. Draw a 2x2 grid labeled Passion, Mission, Vocation, Profession.
2. In 5 minutes, list 6 items in each quadrant.
3. Spend 5 minutes circling overlaps and write one sentence summarizing the overlap.
Expected outcome: 1–2 candidate focal areas to explore.

Exercise 2 — 10-minute Energy ROI quick audit
1. Open last week's calendar. For each multi-hour block, estimate satisfaction 1–5.
2. Note 3 items with lowest satisfaction per hour.
3. Pick one to cut this week and schedule the freed time for a creative experiment.
Expected outcome: immediate reallocation of 1–3 hours with a plan to test.

## Anti-Hallucination
- [VERIFIED] Ikigai intersection and Odyssey Plans are widely used in life-design practice.
- [COMMON-PRACTICE] Time-budgeting and energy audits are common treatments for alignment issues.
- [INFERRED] Energy ROI approximations using subjective scales are useful for prioritization but are not precise.
