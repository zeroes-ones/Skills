## Quickstart (30s outputs)

1) Morning: write 1 line of intention using dichotomy of control (what is in my control today?).
2) Midday: 1 minute of negative visualization—list one small thing that could go wrong and your response.
3) Evening: 3 bullet reflections — what went well, what was outside control, what you will pre-commit to tomorrow.

<!-- STANDARD: 3min --> Ground Rules

- Mechanical triggers:
  - Use dichotomy of control before every emotionally charged conversation.
  - Trigger voluntary discomfort once a week for 30–60 minutes to build tolerance.
  - Use the Stoic test for decisions: if outside control, deprioritize cognitive and emotional investments.
- Negative constraints:
  - Do not use Stoicism to suppress emotion; instead, name feelings and act according to values.
  - Do not weaponize Stoicism as a moralizing tool in teams.
  - Avoid overuse of voluntary discomfort that harms health or relationships.

## Core Workflow

STANDARD: (daily, 5–15 minutes)
1. Morning (3–5 min): intention using dichotomy of control, 1 chosen virtue to practice.
2. Midday micro-check (1–3 min): negative visualization or voluntary discomfort reminder.
3. Evening journaling (5 min): what I controlled, what I learned, what to pre-commit.

DEEP: (weekly/monthly, 30–120 minutes)
1. 7-day Stoic practice protocol (below) with measurable habit tracking.
2. Role ethics review: map top 3 roles and duties; create a 1-page role guide.
3. Integration with CBT: convert automatic thoughts into evidence-based rebuttals.

<!-- DEEP: 10+min --> 7-day Stoic practice protocol

Day 1: Clarify roles & values (60 min)
- Write top 5 roles. For each role, list 3 duties and one stoic virtue to emphasize.
- Outcome: 1-page role ethics guide.

Day 2: Dichotomy of Control drill (30 min)
- Take 5 current worries. For each, label control category: internal / external / mixed.
- Create one micro-action for items in internal control.

Day 3: Negative visualization + pre-mortem (45 min)
- Visualize 3 plausible negative outcomes; for each, write immediate mitigation and emotion management steps.

Day 4: Voluntary discomfort session (60 min)
- Cold shower / fasting window / focused hard physical task. Log subjective tolerance and insights.

Day 5: View-from-above meditation (30 min)
- Practice perspective-taking: trace effects of your actions across time and stakeholders.

Day 6: Amor Fati practice (45 min)
- Reframe a past setback into learning. Write a short paragraph of acceptance + asset reallocation.

Day 7: Integration & commitment (60 min)
- Synthesize notes, set 3-week habit plan, pair with accountability partner for weekly check-ins.

Failure narratives & edge cases

- Misuse case: Using Stoicism to ignore systemic injustice. Fix: combine Stoic acceptance of outcomes with Stoic duty to act when action can reduce harm.
- Emotional suppression: If journaling shows numbing, introduce CBT and expressive writing to process emotions before applying Stoic reasoning.
- Relationship damage: If voluntary discomfort harms partner, scale back intensity and negotiate boundaries.
- Stoic over-rationalization: treating feelings purely as obstacles can miss actionable signals (e.g., chronic fatigue signals burnout). Fix: add health and therapy checkpoints.
- Cultural mismatch: Stoic routines framed as individual toughness can clash with collectivist workplaces. Fix: adapt language to community values and emphasize shared duties.

Advanced practice protocol: Workplace Stoic adaptation
1. Pre-meeting ritual: 60s intention using dichotomy of control and 1 desired virtue.
2. Conflict script: Pause -> Name emotion -> State perspective -> Propose one small constructive action.
3. Team pre-mortem: 15-minute group negative visualization to surface risks before big launches.
4. Leadership stoic audit (quarterly): evaluate decisions leaders made under stress; check for bias towards control illusions or moralizing.

Integration with other frameworks
- Pair negative visualization (Stoic) with a pre-mortem (decision-engineer) to generate action-trigger pairs.
- Use CBT reframing to translate Stoic acceptance into evidence-based behavior change.
- Combine role ethics (Stoic) with stakeholder mapping (ethics-architect) when organizational duties conflict.

Advanced practice: resilience ladder protocol (monthly)
1. Baseline: measure reactivity and recovery time.
2. Progressive exposure: schedule voluntary discomforts of increasing intensity (micro-uncomfortable acts to 60-min sessions) with recovery days.
3. Reflection: after each exposure, journal the thought-action gap and update coping scripts.

Edge cases where Stoicism breaks down
- Complex systemic harms where acceptance without action perpetuates injustice. Counter: pair Stoic acceptance with duty-bound action planning.
- Clinical depression/anxiety: Stoic practices alone may be insufficient; integrate with clinical therapy and medication where indicated.

Practitioner safety rules
- Never push voluntary discomfort beyond medically safe bounds; consult physician for health conditions.
- Make relational consent explicit before involving partners in discomfort protocols.

## Error Decoder

| Error / Pitfall | Root Cause | Fix | Lesson |
|-----------------|------------|-----|--------|
| Stoic as coldness | Misreading Stoic aim | Practice naming emotion + compassionate action | Stoic = regulated feeling, not suppression |
| Overuse of discomfort | Identity signaling | Replace with informative, not punitive, tests | Use discomfort for information gain |
| Moralizing others | Stoic elitism | Bring humility, model practices privately | Avoid using Stoicism to judge others |
| Ignoring systems | Personal stoic focus only | Add civic duty review to role ethics | Stoicism includes duty to reduce harm |
| Journaling drop-off | Low friction design | Use 1-line prompts and calendar triggers | Keep rituals short and consistent |
| Stoic rationalization of abuse | Misapplied acceptance | Escalate to external counsel and prioritize victim safety | Acceptance doesn't excuse harm |
| Health-risking discomfort | Over-enthusiasm without limits | Set medical boundaries and consent rules | Rules protect safety and relations |
| Relationship alienation | Applying practices publically without consent | Reframe privately and seek partner input | Share practices with empathy and invitation |

## Best Practices

1. Start with 1 small ritual for 21 days before adding more and log adherence.
2. Use the dichotomy of control question before any decision that causes stress; write the control classification explicitly.
3. Combine Stoic practice with CBT techniques when negative beliefs persist; track evidence for and against automatic thoughts.
4. Reserve voluntary discomfort for information and tolerance-building; cap intensity and frequency (max once weekly unless medically cleared).
5. Use role ethics to resolve duty conflicts; write 1-page guides per role and test them in conversation with stakeholders.
6. Avoid public moralizing; share practices with consent and invitation; solicit feedback from affected parties.
7. Keep evening reflection concise: 3 bullets only (wins, learnings, commit) and tag one item for improvement tomorrow.
8. Measure resilience metrics: average reactivity (self-rated) and recovery time over 30 days; set a baseline and goal.
9. Use pre-mortem on major projects; invite 3 outside perspectives and document learnings.
10. Pair with an accountability partner for weekly check-ins and a quarterly review.
11. Use small experiments (micro-habits) to scale practices; expand only when adherence >70% for 21 days.
12. Include health and relational safety checks before escalating voluntary discomfort exercises.

## Exercises (5–15 minutes)

Exercise 1: Dichotomy micro-drill (5 min)
1. List 5 worries.
2. Label internal/external/mixed.
3. For one internal item, list one tiny action.

Exercise 2: Negative visualization quick (10 min)
1. Imagine one likely small failure and list 5 concrete impacts.
2. For each impact, write an immediate mitigation step and an emotional anchor phrase to use when it occurs.

Exercise 3: 7-day Stoic challenge (5–15 min per day)
- Day A (Morning): 2-minute intention using dichotomy of control + commit to one virtue.
- Day A (Midday): 2-minute negative visualization of a small, plausible setback + a mitigation step.
- Day A (Evening): 5-minute reflection: what was in your control, what you learned, one gratitude line.
- Repeat daily with incremental stretching: add 1 minute to voluntary discomfort on day 4 and 6 (within safety limits).
- Expected outcome: measurable habit adherence and at least one documented behavioral change after 7 days.

Exercise 4: Partner debrief (10 min)
1. Pair with an accountability partner.
2. Share one Stoic script you plan to use this week and role-play a stressful scenario for 5 minutes.
3. Receive 3 lines of feedback and adjust script.

## Verification: how to measure progress

- Resilience score: self-rate reactivity (1–10) after triggers over 30 days; target decrease by 2 points.
- Habit adherence: % of mornings/evenings logged over 30 days; target >= 70%.
- Social cost check: number of relationship complaints referencing "stoic coldness" over 90 days; target 0.

## What Good Looks Like

- Daily rituals performed ~70% of days for 90 days.
