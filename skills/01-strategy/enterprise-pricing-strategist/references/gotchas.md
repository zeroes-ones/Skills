# Extended Gotchas — Enterprise Pricing Disasters

## 15 Enterprise Pricing Failure Patterns

### 1. The MFN Contamination Cascade
**Scenario:** Growth-stage SaaS signs MFN clause with first $200K enterprise deal. Three years later, 50+ enterprise deals reference this MFN — every deal must match the lowest price given to anyone.
**Cost:** $500K-$2M in destroyed pricing power across entire deal book.
**Prevention:** Never sign MFN. If unavoidable, scope narrowly (same product, volume, geo, 12-month window).

### 2. The Compliance Gap Deal-Killer
**Scenario:** Enterprise sales team closes $750K deal. Legal discovers no SOC 2 report exists. Deal stalls for 8 months waiting for audit completion. Champion's company undergoes reorg — deal dies.
**Cost:** $750K in lost ACV + $100K in wasted sales cycle + competitive intelligence leak.
**Prevention:** Build compliance package before first enterprise hire. SOC 2 Type II takes 6-12 months.

### 3. The Price Transparency Trap
**Scenario:** Enterprise pricing ($100K+) published next to SMB pricing ($99/mo) on website. Every enterprise prospect anchors at SMB pricing. Average discount from list: 60%.
**Cost:** $200K-$800K/year in foregone revenue per enterprise deal.
**Prevention:** "Contact Sales" for enterprise. Remove all price anchors. Discovery reveals budget.

### 4. The Discount Spiral Death Loop
**Scenario:** VP Sales incentivized on closed deals, not margin. AEs learn "every deal gets 40% off." Discount becomes embedded in sales culture. New AEs trained that "list price is fake."
**Cost:** $500K-$2M/year in margin erosion across all deals.
**Prevention:** Approval matrix with hard gates. Track discount-given vs quota. Coach outliers.

### 5. The Unilateral SLA Obliteration
**Scenario:** Enterprise contract promises 99.99% uptime with uncapped credits. One 8-hour outage = $100K+ in credits, wiping out months of margin. No force majeure exclusion for cloud provider outages.
**Cost:** $100K-$500K per major incident.
**Prevention:** Cap SLA credits at 25-50% monthly fees. Force majeure for upstream provider outages.

### 6. The International Legal Quagmire
**Scenario:** US-based MSA used for German enterprise deal. GDPR violation found in data handling. German DPA fines 4% of global revenue.
**Cost:** $200K-$1M+ in fines + remediation + reputation damage.
**Prevention:** Local counsel review for every jurisdiction. Local entity or compliant international MSA.

### 7. The Anchor Customer Dependency
**Scenario:** One customer represents 40% of enterprise revenue. They demand 50% discount at renewal or they walk. You have no leverage — they know your revenue dependency.
**Cost:** $300K-$1M in forced discount or churn.
**Prevention:** No single customer > 15% of total revenue. Diversify enterprise customer base.

### 8. The Feature Giveaway Erosion
**Scenario:** SSO and audit logs added to "Better" tier to close a deal. Now all Better-tier customers demand same features. Enterprise tier loses its primary differentiator.
**Cost:** $200K-$500K in downgraded enterprise deals.
**Prevention:** Enterprise feature gates are non-negotiable. Never move them down-tier.

### 9. The Renewal Surprise Massacre
**Scenario:** Enterprise customer on legacy pricing ($50K/year). Market price is now $120K/year. At renewal, you propose 140% increase. Customer walks, writes scathing G2 review.
**Cost:** Churned $50K + negative reference + G2 damage.
**Presence:** 5-7% annual escalator built into multi-year contracts. Gradual convergence to market price.

### 10. The Payment Term Cash Crunch
**Scenario:** Multiple enterprise deals signed with Net-90 payment terms. $2M in revenue booked but zero cash collected. Can't make payroll.
**Cost:** Cash flow crisis, potential bridge financing at predatory rates.
**Prevention:** Annual upfront billing standard. Net-30 maximum for exceptions. Track cash collected, not just bookings.

### 11. The Scope Creep Enterprise Deal
**Scenario:** Standard enterprise deal expands scope during negotiation: custom integrations, dedicated infrastructure, 24/7 support. Original pricing didn't account for any of this. Deal closes at original price with 3x delivery cost.
**Cost:** $100K-$500K in negative margin on deal + distracts engineering from roadmap.
**Prevention:** Scope changes = quote revisions. Every new requirement triggers pricing review.

### 12. The Champion Churn Catastrophe
**Scenario:** Enterprise champion (VP who bought your product) leaves company. New VP has preferred vendor relationship. Your renewal is dead before you're aware.
**Cost:** $200K-$1M in lost account AND competitor now entrenched.
**Prevention:** Multi-thread relationships. Never rely on single champion. Executive sponsor + CSM + end-user advocates.

### 13. The Public Sector Quicksand
**Scenario:** Government agency loves your product. 18-month procurement process. Requires FedRAMP certification (not mentioned until legal review). Deal dies after $150K invested in pursuit.
**Cost:** $150K in wasted pursuit + opportunity cost.
**Prevention:** Qualify procurement requirements on first call. "What certifications are required for your purchasing process?"

### 14. The Indirect Channel Deception
**Scenario:** Reseller promises 3 enterprise deals in 6 months. Zero deals materialize. 12-month exclusivity clause blocks your direct sales team from the territory.
**Cost:** $200K-$400K in lost territory revenue.
**Prevention:** Performance-based exclusivity. Reseller must meet quarterly quotas or lose exclusivity.

### 15. The Billing System Limitation Breakdown
**Scenario:** Signed a complex usage-based enterprise deal with tiered pricing, quarterly true-ups, and multi-currency. Your billing system can't handle it. Manual invoicing for 12 months. Errors = customer trust damage.
**Cost:** $50K-$200K in billing errors + customer service costs + trust erosion.
**Presence:** Validate billing system supports the pricing model before signing the deal.
