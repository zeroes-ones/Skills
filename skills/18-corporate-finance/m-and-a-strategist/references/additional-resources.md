## Deal-Structure Playbooks

### LOI / Term Sheet Core Terms
| Term | Typical Range | What Moves Effective Value |
|------|---------------|----------------------------|
| Exclusivity | 30-60 days | Longer exclusivity without progress = seller lock-in; negotiate a diligence-results gate |
| Escrow | 5-15% of price, 12-24 months | Covers post-close indemnity claims; negotiate release schedule tied to survival periods |
| Indemnity cap | 10-50% of price (business reps); 100% (fundamental reps) | The cap is the real risk ceiling — negotiate scope, not just the number |
| Survival period | 12-24 months business reps; statutory for fundamental | Longer survival = more seller exposure; align with where risk actually lives |
| Working-capital peg | Trailing 12-month average or closing-date target | A loose peg lets the seller strip cash pre-close; define components + verification in the LOI |
| Earnout | 10-40% of price, 1-3 years, capped | Only for genuine uncertainty; tie to controllable metrics with accounting definitions |
| Seller rollover | 0-30% equity | Aligns seller incentives post-close; negotiate vesting and board/observer rights |

### QoE Work Program (buy-side)
1. Recompute EBITDA from GL, not from the seller's bridge.
2. Verify each add-back: owner salary, one-time costs, related-party items, pro-forma adjustments.
3. Test revenue quality: top-10 customers, contract vs cash, renewal history, churn assumptions.
4. Recompute working capital at the peg date using the LOI methodology.
5. Produce the normalized-EBITDA bridge with every line tagged [VERIFIED]/[ESTIMATED].

## War Stories

- **The add-back that cost $2M:** Seller claimed $2M of "non-recurring founder costs" as add-backs. Buyer priced at 11x without recomputing. Post-close, $1.5M of those costs reoccurred (founder's family on payroll, related-party leases). Lesson: every add-back is priced at the multiple — a $2M error at 11x is a $22M pricing error. Recompute from the GL, always.
- **The auction that needed a walk-away:** Two disciplined buyers exited a process at $70M. The winner paid $90M — exactly where the other two stopped. The winner's thesis required the cost discipline that the price made impossible. Lesson: when informed buyers walk, their exit is information. Ask why before you bid past them.
- **The day-1 departure:** Target's top salesperson and CTO left within a month. Retention had been assessed at the org level ("culture seems good"). The pipeline and roadmap left with them. Lesson: retention is per-person for the top 10%, signed before close.
