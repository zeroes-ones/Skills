# Leadership Ladder Interviews — EM to CTO Question Banks & Cases

> Deep reference for `engineering-leadership-interview-prep`. Level-by-level scope, question
> banks, answer frameworks, and case formats. Content is original guidance (research-source:
> own knowledge) — pair with the role skills (engineering-manager, director-engineering,
> vp-engineering, cto-advisor) for job depth.

## Level scope cheat-sheet

| Level | Scope | Span | Cases they run | Who interviews |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| EM | Team | 5-10 ICs, quarters | People management, delivery | Director, peer EMs, a report or two |
| Sr EM | Multi-team | 2-4 teams / 10-30, EMs reporting | People + process + delivery at multi-team scope | Director, peers |
| Director | Org | 20-200, managers of managers | Org design, EM bench, cross-functional | VP, peer directors, execs |
| Sr Director | Large org | 100-400+, multiple orgs | Org at scale, strategy translation | VP/SVP, execs |
| VP Eng / SVP | Company function | 50-1000+ | Strategy, exec alignment, board comms | CTO/CEO, execs, sometimes board |
| CTO | Company + tech vision | Whole company | Vision, platform bets, M&A, board/investors | CEO, board |

## Question bank by level

### EM / Senior EM (people-management track)
- Tell me about a time you grew an engineer from mid to senior. (Growth + method)
- How do you run 1:1s that actually matter? (Process + empathy)
- Walk me through how you handled an underperformer — from first sign to outcome. (Process + courage)
- Tell me about a time you had to fire someone. What did you do to make it dignified? (Courage + empathy + legal)
- How do you hire your first N engineers, and what did you look for? (Hiring bar)
- Tell me about a conflict between two reports and how you resolved it. (Empathy + fairness)
- A key engineer wants to leave. What do you do? (Retention)
- How do you handle your own burnout or a report's burnout? (Self-awareness)
- Tell me about a delivery that slipped badly. What happened and what did you change? (Ownership + failure)
- How do you manage up when your director wants faster than your team can go? (Managing up + trade-offs)

### Director / Senior Director (org-design track)
- Our org is 40 engineers across feature teams and delivery is slowing. How would you restructure? (Org-design case)
- When do you add a manager layer, and how do you know the org is ready? (Org design)
- How do you develop your EM bench? What do you do when a new EM struggles? (EM development)
- Tell me about a reorg you ran — how did you decide, communicate, and handle the fallout? (Change leadership)
- How do you partner with product and design so engineering has a seat at the table? (Cross-functional)
- Walk me through your headcount planning and budget defense. (Business framing)
- An acquisition's team is joining your org. How do you integrate them? (Integration)
- How do you keep quality and velocity both moving at 100+ engineers? (Systems at scale)
- Tell me about a time your org missed a big commitment. (Ownership + failure at org scope)
- How do you handle a director-level peer who's blocking your org? (Exec conflict)

### VP / SVP / Head of Engineering (strategy track)
- Where should engineering be in 3 years, and what do you stop doing to get there? (Strategy case)
- How do you set the engineering strategy so it ties to revenue and customer outcomes? (Business framing)
- The CEO wants 2x delivery with the same team. What do you say? (Exec communication + trade-offs)
- Tell me about a time you had to defend a major platform bet to the exec team. (Strategic conviction)
- How do you report engineering health to the board? What metrics, and why those? (Board comms)
- Walk me through a layoff or restructuring you led. How did you decide and communicate? (Courage + empathy at scale)
- How do you build a leadership team under you? What do you look for in a Director hire? (Talent at scale)
- How do you handle an exec who wants you to compromise on quality for a date? (Standing your ground)
- Tell me about a technical debt problem that became a business problem on your watch. (Ownership)
- How do you run an M&A technical due diligence? (Business + technical judgment)

### CTO (vision track)
- What's your technology vision for a company like ours, and how does it create business advantage? (Vision)
- Build vs buy vs partner for our core platform — walk me through your framework. (Judgment)
- How do you evaluate a potential acquisition's technology? What kills a deal? (M&A diligence)
- Where does AI fit in our product and our engineering org in the next 3 years? (Forward-looking)
- How do you keep a 500-engineer org innovating instead of just maintaining? (Org culture at scale)
- The board asks why we're spending 20% of revenue on engineering. What's your answer? (Board + business)
- Tell me about a technology bet you made that failed. What did it cost and what changed? (Failure + judgment)
- How do you hire and manage a VP of Engineering? (Executive leadership)
- How do you handle a CTO peer (product, CFO) disagreeing with your roadmap? (Exec dynamics)
- What would you change in our engineering org in your first 90 days? (Action plan)

## Answer frameworks

### STAR with metrics (behavioral)
Situation (context, 2 lines) → Task (your job) → Action (what YOU did, 60% of the answer) → Result (metric + people impact). Always end with the outcome number and who benefited.

### Underperformer process (people problems)
1. Diagnose (skill, will, or circumstance?) → 2. Listen (their perspective) → 3. Coach with a written plan and clear expectations → 4. Document consistently → 5. Act (improve, redeploy, or exit with dignity). Empathy and legal awareness at every step.

### Org-design case (Director)
1. Requirements (business goals, headcount, constraints, team maturity) → 2. Principles (what the org must optimize: delivery, ownership, learning) → 3. Options (2-3 structures with trade-offs) → 4. Recommendation + why → 5. Risks + how you'd measure success. Draw it; name what you gave up.

### Strategy case (VP/CTO)
1. Business context (where the company is going) → 2. Engineering gaps (what blocks the business) → 3. Strategic bets (2-3, each tied to a business outcome) → 4. Roadmap + metrics → 5. Risks and dependencies. Every bet answers "so what for the business?"

### Hostile/ambiguous exec question
1. Stay calm; pause. → 2. Restate the question neutrally ("So you're asking whether…") → 3. Answer the real question with data. → 4. Hold your ground with a reason, offer a path. Composure is the answer.

### 90-day plan (level-scoped)
- **EM:** weeks 1-2 listen (1:1s, codebase, delivery), 3-4 quick wins, 5-8 build trust + set cadence, 9-13 first people moves.
- **Director:** weeks 1-4 org audit (who delivers, EM bench, bottlenecks), 5-8 align with peers, 9-13 first org/process changes with metrics.
- **VP/CTO:** weeks 1-4 business + exec alignment, strategy draft, 5-8 present vision, 9-13 first bets + org changes + board communication.

## Mock scoring rubric

| Axis | Weight | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| People impact | 20 | Did the answer show effect on team/org/people? |
| Method + metrics | 20 | Was there a how and a number? |
| Level scope | 15 | Team, org, or company — matching the target? |
| Trade-offs | 15 | Did they name an option they rejected and why? |
| Empathy | 10 | Did they show the other side's perspective? |
| Credit-sharing | 10 | Team wins, or hero narrative? |
| Business framing | 10 | Tied to business outcomes (Director+) or clear (EM)? |
