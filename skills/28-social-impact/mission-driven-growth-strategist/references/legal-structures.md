# Legal Structures for Mission-Driven Organizations

## Comparison Matrix

| Feature | Benefit Corp | B Corp Certification | L3C | CIC (UK) | Cooperative | Steward-Ownership |
|---------|-------------|---------------------|-----|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **Legal Status** | Yes (state law) | No (certification) | Yes (state law) | Yes (UK law) | Yes (varies) | Contractual/trust |
| **Jurisdiction** | 40+ US states | Global (any entity) | Limited US states | UK only | Global (varies) | Global |
| **Mission Lock** | Charter clause | Legal amendment required | Low-profit purpose | Asset lock + community test | Member bylaws | Golden share/trust |
| **Fiduciary Duty** | Stakeholder-inclusive | Depends on entity type | Charitable/educational purpose only | Community interest | Member benefit | Purpose-first |

## Jurisdiction Requirements

### United States

**Benefit Corporation:**
- Available in: DE, CA, NY, CO, OR, PA, and 35+ other states (+ DC)
- NOT available in: AL, AK, GA, IA, KS, MS, NM, NC, ND, OH, OK, SD, WV, WY (check current status)
- Requirement: Public benefit purpose in charter, annual benefit report against third-party standard
- Key states: DE (corporate-friendly, public benefit corporation), CA (strictest reporting requirements)

**L3C (Low-Profit Limited Liability Company):**
- Available in: IL, LA, ME, MI, RI, UT, VT, WY (+ formerly NC, now repealed)
- Requirement: Charitable/educational purpose, no significant profit purpose, no political purpose
- Key feature: Designed for program-related investments (PRIs) from foundations
- Decline: IL and other states have sunset provisions; L3C adoption has been limited

**Flexible Purpose Corporation:**
- California only (Corporations Code §2500-3503)
- Allows for-profit corporation with special purpose beyond shareholder value
- Less common than benefit corporation; CA now also offers benefit corporation

### United Kingdom

**Community Interest Company (CIC):**
- Register with Companies House + CIC Regulator approval
- Requirements: Community Interest Test (reasonable person would consider activities for community benefit), Asset Lock (assets can only be used for community purposes), Dividend Cap (35% of profits maximum to shareholders)
- Annual CIC report: what was done for community benefit, stakeholder consultation

### Global

**B Corp Certification:**
- Available in 80+ countries, 7,000+ certified companies
- 80+ points on B Impact Assessment (200-point max)
- Legal accountability amendment: board must consider stakeholder impact
- Recertification every 3 years

**Cooperative:**
- ICA (International Cooperative Alliance) principles: voluntary membership, democratic member control, member economic participation, autonomy and independence, education/training, cooperation among cooperatives, concern for community
- Legal form varies by country: worker cooperative, consumer cooperative, multi-stakeholder cooperative, platform cooperative

**Steward-Ownership:**
- Two principles: (1) Self-governance — voting rights held by people connected to the mission (not capital markets), (2) Profits serve purpose — profits reinvested in mission, not extracted by shareholders
- Implementation: Golden share held by trust/foundation with veto power over mission changes, profit distribution waterfall prioritizing reinvestment and stakeholder benefit
- Examples: Patagonia (Purpose Trust + Holdfast Collective), Bosch (Robert Bosch Stiftung), Carlsberg (Carlsberg Foundation)

## Decision Framework

### Choose Benefit Corporation if:
- Raising venture capital (investors understand corporate form)
- Operating in a state with enabling legislation
- Want enforceable mission-lock through charter and shareholder lawsuit rights
- Need to balance investor returns with mission

### Choose B Corp Certification if:
- Want globally recognized third-party verification
- Entity type (LLC, C-corp, etc.) already exists and conversion is costly
- Funders/investors care about B Corp status as a signal
- Can commit to recertification every 3 years

### Choose CIC (UK) if:
- Operating primarily in the UK
- Asset lock is essential to mission
- Can accept dividend cap
- Funders require community interest legal protection

### Choose Cooperative if:
- Beneficiaries/users should own and govern
- Democratic governance is core to the model
- Operating in a sector with cooperative precedent (agriculture, credit, housing, platform)
- Can invest in member education and governance capacity

### Choose Steward-Ownership if:
- Founder wants to exit without selling mission
- Want permanent separation of money and power
- Raising capital through debt/royalty rather than equity
- Long-term independence is the goal (not acquisition or IPO)

## Structural Conversion Paths

### LLC/C-Corp → Benefit Corporation
1. Board resolution approving conversion
2. Shareholder vote (typically 2/3 majority for C-corp)
3. File conversion documents with Secretary of State
4. Amend charter to include public benefit purpose
5. Update bylaws to reflect stakeholder governance

### Any Entity → B Corp Certification
1. Complete B Impact Assessment (target 80+)
2. Adopt legal accountability amendment (board resolution + bylaws change)
3. Sign B Corp Declaration of Interdependence and Term Sheet
4. B Lab verification call and document review
5. Pay certification fee ($500-$50,000 based on revenue)
6. Recertify every 3 years

### Failing to protect mission at exit (conversion risk):
- Without legal protection, acquirer can convert benefit corporation to standard corporation
- B Corp certification can be dropped immediately upon acquisition
- Solution: Include mission preservation covenants in acquisition agreement AND steward-ownership overlay
