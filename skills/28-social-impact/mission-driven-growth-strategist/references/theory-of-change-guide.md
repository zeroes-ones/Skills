# Theory of Change Guide

## What is a Theory of Change?

A Theory of Change (ToC) is a comprehensive description and illustration of *how and why* a desired change is expected to happen in a particular context. It maps the journey from activities to impact through a causal chain of preconditions.

### ToC vs Logical Framework (Logframe)

| Dimension | Theory of Change | Logical Framework |
|-----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| **Format** | Narrative + diagram | Matrix (4x4 grid) |
| **Focus** | Why change happens | What will be delivered |
| **Logic** | Backward mapping from impact | Forward planning from activities |
| **Assumptions** | Explicit, tested | Implicit, stated but not tested |
| **Use** | Strategy design, learning | Project management, M&E reporting |
| **Flexibility** | Living document, revised with evidence | Fixed at project start |

## Backward Mapping Process

### Step 1: Define Long-Term Impact

Format: **[Specific population] achieves [specific outcome] at [measurable level] by [timeframe].**

**Example:** "10,000 smallholder coffee farmers in Rwanda achieve 40% income increase and household food security by 2030."

### Step 2: Identify Outcomes (Preconditions)

For each outcome, ask: "What must be true for this outcome to occur?"

Map backward:

```

Impact ← Outcome 1 ← Outcome 2 ← ... ← Outputs ← Activities
```

**Example chain:**

```

Farmers achieve 40% income increase
  ← Farmers access premium markets (Outcome 3)
    ← Farmers produce specialty-grade coffee (Outcome 2)
      ← Farmers adopt improved agricultural practices (Outcome 1)
        ← Training program delivered (Output)
          ← Curriculum development + farmer workshops (Activities)
```

### Step 3: Name Assumptions

For each arrow in the ToC:

**Format:** "If [output/outcome], then [next outcome], because [assumption]."

**Example:** "If farmers complete training (output), then they will adopt improved practices (outcome), because the practices demonstrably increase yield without requiring additional capital investment (assumption)."

### Step 4: Rate Assumptions

| Rating | Definition | Action |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Strong** | Supported by multiple published studies or RCTs | Monitor only |
| **Moderate** | Supported by one study or strong practitioner consensus | Periodic verification |
| **Weak** | Logical argument only, no empirical evidence | Priority testing |
| **Unknown** | Pure hypothesis, no evidence exists | Immediate testing |

### Step 5: Define Indicators

For each outcome, select at least one measurable indicator:

| Outcome | Indicator | Data Source | Frequency |
|---------|-----------|-------------|-----------|
| Farmers adopt improved practices | % of trained farmers using new techniques within 6 months | Field observation + farmer self-report | Quarterly |
| Farmers produce specialty-grade coffee | % of harvest scoring 80+ on SCA cupping scale | Third-party cupping lab | Annually |
| Farmers access premium markets | % of coffee sold at >Fair Trade minimum price | Sales records | Annually |

## Stakeholder Workshop Facilitation Guide

### Duration: 1-2 days

### Participants
- Program leadership (decision-makers)
- Frontline staff (implementation reality)
- Beneficiary representatives (lived experience)
- External expert/facilitator (neutral perspective)
- Potentially: funders, partners, skeptics

### Agenda

**Morning Session (3 hours): Defining Impact**
1. Icebreaker: "What does success look like in 10 years?" (30 min)
2. Long-term impact statement drafting (60 min)
3. Stakeholder perspectives: each group describes their vision (45 min)
4. Consensus building: combine perspectives into one impact statement (45 min)

**Afternoon Session (3 hours): Backward Mapping**
5. Brainstorm: "What must be true for this impact to occur?" — all preconditions (60 min)
6. Organize preconditions into causal chain (60 min)
7. Name assumptions for each arrow (45 min)
8. Rate assumptions: Strong/Moderate/Weak/Unknown (15 min)

**Day 2 Morning (3 hours): Indicators & Validation**
9. Define indicators for each outcome (60 min)
10. Design data collection methods (60 min)
11. Plan assumption testing: which Weak/Unknown assumptions to test first (45 min)
12. Assign outcome owners and review cadence (15 min)

### Facilitation Tips
- Use sticky notes for preconditions — they're easier to reorganize
- Insist on specificity: "improved livelihoods" → "30% income increase from agriculture"
- Capture disagreements rather than resolving them — disagreements are untested assumptions
- Document the final ToC as both diagram AND narrative — different audiences need different formats

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Forward design:** Starting with activities and working forward to impact. This justifies existing programs rather than identifying the most effective path.
2. **Overly linear thinking:** Assuming a simple chain when reality has feedback loops, multiple pathways, and interaction effects.
3. **Assumption blindness:** Not naming assumptions because they feel "obvious." Every unexamined assumption is a potential failure point.
4. **Indicator overload:** Defining 50 indicators and collecting none of them well. Start with 5-10 core indicators.
5. **One-and-done:** Treating ToC as a deliverable rather than a management tool. Review quarterly against outcome data.
