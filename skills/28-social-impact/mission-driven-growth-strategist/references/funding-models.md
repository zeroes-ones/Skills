# Funding Models for Mission-Driven Organizations

## Capital Spectrum

```
Low Return Expectation                          High Return Expectation
←────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────→
Philanthropic    Program-Related    Impact Investment   Commercial
Grants          Investments (PRI)   (Concessionary)     Investment
(0%)            (1-3%)              (3-7%)              (8-15%+)
```

| Capital Type | Source | Return Expected | Risk Tolerance | Reporting |
|-------------|--------|----------------|----------------|-----------|
| **Philanthropic Grants** | Foundations, governments, individuals | None | Highest | Outcome reports |
| **PRIs** | Foundations (counts toward 5% distribution) | 1-3% | High | Social + financial |
| **Concessionary Impact** | Impact funds, DFIs | 3-7% | Medium-high | IRIS+ metrics |
| **Market-Rate Impact** | Impact funds, ESG funds | 7-12% | Medium | IRIS+ + financial |
| **Commercial Investment** | VCs, banks, public markets | 12%+ | Low-medium | Financial only |

## Blended Finance Capital Stack Waterfall

### Standard Waterfall Structure

```
Senior Debt
  ↑ (paid first, lowest risk)
  └─ Commercial banks, DFI senior tranche, guarantee-backed
     Return: 4-7%, First claim on cash flows

Mezzanine / Subordinated Debt
  ↑ (paid after senior)
  └─ Impact funds, foundation PRIs
     Return: 6-10%, Absorbs losses after equity but before senior

Equity / First-Loss Capital
  ↑ (paid last, absorbs first losses)
  └─ Catalytic philanthropy, concessionary impact investors
     Return: 0-5% (or negative if losses), Enables senior capital
```

### Waterfall Documentation Template

**For each tranche, specify:**

1. **Capital Amount**: How much?
2. **Capital Source**: Foundation, impact fund, commercial bank, etc.
3. **Return Expectation**: Fixed rate, variable, equity appreciation
4. **Risk Position**: Where in the waterfall? Who gets paid first/last?
5. **Governance Rights**: Board seat? Observer? Veto rights over specific decisions?
6. **Exit Mechanics**: Repayment schedule, conversion rights, put/call options
7. **Mission Alignment**: How does this capital support vs constrain the mission?
8. **Covenants**: Financial covenants (debt service coverage ratio, liquidity), impact covenants (outcome targets)

## Revenue Model Archetypes

### 1. Fee-for-Service
**Description:** Charge beneficiaries (or third-party payers) for services delivered.
**Examples:** Healthcare clinics, microfinance institutions, training programs.
**Pros:** Direct beneficiary relationship, earned revenue is unrestricted.
**Cons:** Can exclude poorest beneficiaries who can't pay.
**Mitigation:** Sliding scale, cross-subsidization (higher fees from wealthy clients subsidize free services).

### 2. Product Sales
**Description:** Sell physical or digital products that advance the mission.
**Examples:** Fair trade goods, solar lanterns, educational apps.
**Pros:** Scalable, market validation of impact.
**Cons:** Supply chain complexity, competition with commercial alternatives.
**Mitigation:** Premium pricing for impact story, B2B sales to institutions rather than B2C.

### 3. Licensing / Franchising
**Description:** License the model to other organizations.
**Examples:** VisionSpring's vision entrepreneur model, BRAC's replication.
**Pros:** High scalability, lower capital requirements.
**Cons:** Quality control, mission drift by licensees.
**Mitigation:** Certification requirements, ongoing monitoring, brand protection.

### 4. Marketplace / Platform
**Description:** Connect buyers and sellers, take a transaction fee.
**Examples:** Fair trade marketplaces, gig economy platforms for underserved workers.
**Pros:** Network effects, asset-light.
**Cons:** Chicken-and-egg problem, disintermediation risk.
**Mitigation:** Value-added services (training, certification, financing).

### 5. Data / Insights
**Description:** Sell aggregated, anonymized data or insights generated through mission work.
**Examples:** Health data for research, agricultural data for insurers.
**Pros:** High margin, scalable.
**Cons:** Privacy concerns, beneficiary consent, mission conflict (selling data).
**Mitigation:** Opt-in consent, beneficiary benefit-sharing, transparent data use policies.

### 6. Membership / Subscription
**Description:** Recurring revenue from members who support the mission.
**Examples:** Cooperative memberships, supporter subscriptions.
**Pros:** Predictable revenue, community building.
**Cons:** Limited scale (membership fees are typically small).
**Mitigation:** Tiered membership with premium benefits, corporate membership programs.

## Grant vs Investment Decision Matrix

| Question | If Yes → Grant | If Yes → Investment |
|----------|---------------|---------------------|
| Can you generate revenue from this activity? | No (pure social service) | Yes (earned revenue model) |
| Is the target population able to pay? | No (extreme poverty) | Yes (ability + willingness) |
| Is the outcome monetizable by a third party? | No (public good) | Yes (government/insurer can pay) |
| Is there a clear path to financial sustainability? | No (perpetual subsidy needed) | Yes (path to breakeven) |
| Is the timeline to sustainability >7 years? | Yes | No (>7 years, use grant; <7 years, investment possible) |

## Alternative Instruments

### Pay-for-Success / Social Impact Bonds (SIBs)
- **Mechanism:** Government agrees to pay for verified outcomes; private investors fund the intervention; if outcomes are achieved, government repays investors with a return; if not, investors lose capital.
- **Best for:** Interventions with measurable, attributable outcomes; strong evidence base; government willing to pay for outcomes.
- **Cost:** High transaction costs ($200K-$500K for structuring), requires independent evaluator.
- **Example:** Rikers Island SIB (criminal justice), Peterborough SIB (recidivism reduction).

### Direct Public Offerings (DPOs)
- **Mechanism:** Sell shares or bonds directly to the community (accredited AND non-accredited investors).
- **Best for:** Organizations with strong community brand, local economic development focus.
- **Cost:** $50K-$150K for legal, marketing, and administration.
- **Example:** Equal Exchange (fair trade), Real Pickles (worker-owned food business).

### Revenue-Based Financing
- **Mechanism:** Investor provides capital in exchange for a fixed % of future revenue until a total repayment cap is reached (typically 1.3-1.8x).
- **Best for:** Organizations with predictable revenue, SaaS or recurring revenue models, avoiding equity dilution.
- **Cost:** Effective interest rate 15-30% depending on revenue growth.
- **Example:** Novel, Lighter Capital, SaaS Capital.

### Community Bonds
- **Mechanism:** Community members lend money to the organization via bonds, repaid with interest over time.
- **Best for:** Organizations with strong community trust, local focus.
- **Cost:** $30K-$100K for legal structuring and marketing.
- **Example:** Centre for Social Innovation (Toronto, raised $6.5M via community bonds).

### Concentration Risk Thresholds

- **Red (<10%):** Low risk — monitor annually
- **Yellow (10-20%):** Moderate risk — quarterly review, contingency plan documented
- **Orange (20-30%):** High risk — diversification plan active, monthly monitoring, contingency funded
- **Red (>30%):** Critical risk — active replacement sourcing, board-level monthly review, cash reserves ≥6 months
